Above all, keep the discipline — and the care — that give these efforts their force. Document the poison and the loss. Make the health and livelihoods case. Defend the delta through the river framework. Use the global frameworks. Work carefully through the community. And protect the vulnerable — because in a poor, troubled nation whose water and food are at stake, an effort that reduces the poison and defends the lifeline through health, livelihoods, and the world's frameworks, carried carefully and protecting those who act, is the one that is both effective and just.

Mali is a great and ancient land of the Niger, whose life flows from the river and the vast inland delta at its heart, feeding well over a million people with its fish and floating rice and dry-season pastures, and whose earth is rich with the gold that made its old empires famous — and its environmental struggle is that the same gold now poisons the water with mercury while the delta that feeds the nation is squeezed by dams and drought, in a land under military rule and gripped by a hard conflict. This guide has hidden none of that, nor pretended that gold can be wished away, or that the delta's fate is simple, or that the conditions are safe, and there is no landmark victory to promise you. But the water and food of millions are a stake that even a troubled nation cannot finally ignore, and the levers are real: the global frameworks that can reduce the poison of the gold, the shared governance that can defend the delta, the livelihoods that unite the people, and the world's partners who stand with a poor land. There is no ending the gold that feeds millions, and no easy command of a river that rises beyond the border; this guide has said so throughout, and insisted above all on the safety of those who act. But the water and delta and people of Mali are worth every careful effort, the levers that run through health, the lifeline, and the world's frameworks are real, and the patient, careful, protected work of documenting the poison, making the health case, and defending the delta is one that Malians and their allies can genuinely do — and, carefully and safely, defend the water and food of millions. So can you.

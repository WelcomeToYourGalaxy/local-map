# HOW COMMUNITIES CAN STOP DESTRUCTIVE DEVELOPMENT PROJECTS IN PALAU

*Complete Community Opposition How-To*

*This guide is built around how Palauans turned good science and their own tradition into lasting protection — a community that took reef science to its own leaders and won a ban, then drew on the traditional moratorium called "bul" to protect land, watersheds, and reefs — and it names the tools you'll meet (the land-and-sea science, the "bul" moratorium, the community alliance, the state and traditional leaders) in plain words.*

# INTRODUCTION & FRAMEWORK

When scientists demonstrated that clearing the land — and especially the **mangroves** along the shore — was sending sediment onto the **reefs** and harming the fish that feed Palauan families, the people of one state did something practical and effective: they took that evidence to their own **state government and traditional leaders** and lobbied until the **clearing of mangroves was banned**. And that was not a lone act. Across the largest island, community after community, working together through an **alliance**, drew on Palau's own tradition of the temporary moratorium the islands call **"bul,"** and established their own **protected areas** on the land — turning scientific understanding and cultural practice into lasting protection for the forests, the watersheds, and the reefs below them. The destructive clearing was stopped where the community made its case; the protected areas hold.

The Palauan communities who won a mangrove-clearing ban drew on tools rooted in the islands' own tradition. The distinctive one is **clear science linking land to sea** — the sediment, the reefs, the fish — which gives a community something a leader can act on, as it won the ban. The **traditional "bul" moratorium** — Palau's own custom of temporarily closing an area to let it recover — is a living, culturally-rooted tool for protection. A **community alliance**, communities acting together, multiplies the power, as it did across the largest island. The **state government and traditional leaders**, closest to home, are the people with the power to respond; protest, petition, and lobbying are lawful and safe. And Palau's **strong conservation tradition and national leadership** stand behind it. But these tools have real limits, and this guide states them plainly. Palau's great national acts — the vast **National Marine Sanctuary**, the world-first **shark sanctuary** — were led by national leaders, and a community's power is strongest at the **state and traditional level**, on the land and reefs closest to home. The science must be **clear and credible** to persuade. And the **pressure for development** on small, beautiful islands is real. So aim for what these levers can deliver by arming the community with science and taking it to the leaders closest to home.

## The Whole Strategy in One Idea

Kuwait is a wealthy nation that carries a grievous environmental wound — the oil lakes and contaminated groundwater left when a war set its wells ablaze — and that bears, too, the ordinary burdens of an oil economy: a deep dependence on crude, a fierce and worsening heat, a thirst slaked only by desalination, a stressed and shared Gulf. This guide has hidden none of that, nor pretended that oil can be wished away, or that all the harm is Kuwait's own fault, or that its once-open civic space is undiminished, and there is no single victory to promise you. But Kuwait holds two things many nations in this series do not: the wealth, and an international award, to heal the very land war scarred, so that it cannot plead poverty; and a real, if narrowed, tradition of civic and parliamentary accountability, rare in its region. Those are the levers — the obligation a rich nation is bound and funded to meet, the health of the water it cannot spare, the commitments it has made, the means it plainly has, and the civic voice its tradition allows. There is no ending oil, and no escaping the shared and complex responsibility for the harm; this guide has said so throughout, and insisted on care for those who act. But the land and water and Gulf of Kuwait are worth healing and protecting, the levers that run through a rich nation's obligations and capacity are real, and the patient, careful work of documenting the harm, pressing the remediation owed, and holding a wealthy state to what it can plainly afford is one that Kuwaitis can genuinely do — through the channels that remain — and heal the land and steward its environment. So can you.

Somalia is a Horn of Africa land of acacia woodlands and a long, once-rich coast, worn by decades of conflict and drought — its woodlands felled for a charcoal that funds war and feeds foreign markets, its seas plundered by foreign fleets and poisoned by dumped waste, its land parched and its people driven by desperation into the very trade that deepens their loss. This guide has hidden none of that, nor pretended that the state can yet defend its woodlands and seas, or that the charcoal is simple, or that the drought can be quickly undone, and there is no landmark victory to promise you. But the charcoal is banned by the world, and there its markets can be pressed and its cycle broken by alternatives; the foreign fishing and the dumping are harms the world's maritime frameworks can reach; and the land can be restored and the desperation eased with the world's support. Those are the levers, and they are real: the world's ban and the markets that must close to the charcoal, the maritime frameworks and the partners who patrol these waters, the climate and humanitarian frameworks that restore the land, and the large diaspora and the world's conscience that reach a fragile state from outside. There is no enforcement the state can yet reliably deliver, and no quick end to harms bound up with conflict and a plundered sea; this guide has said so throughout, and insisted above all on the absolute protection of anyone inside. But the woodlands and seas and land of Somalia are worth every careful, international effort, the levers that run through the world's frameworks and the alternatives are real, and the patient, external, protective work of documenting the harm, pressing the ban and the fleets, and building the alternatives is one that the international community, the large diaspora, and the safe communities can genuinely do — and, from outside and with care, help a worn and resilient land begin to defend its woodlands and its seas. So can you.

# HOW COMMUNITIES CAN STOP DESTRUCTIVE DEVELOPMENT PROJECTS IN THE GAMBIA

*Complete Community Opposition How-To*

# INTRODUCTION & FRAMEWORK

Community action shapes what happens to the coast and waters of The Gambia, and one fishing village showed that a community, newly free to speak, can force a powerful foreign polluter to answer for the harm it does — even when the win is partial and the struggle goes on. When a foreign-owned fishmeal factory opened beside the village's lagoon — the country's first community-managed wildlife reserve, tended by local people for more than two decades — and began fouling the water until the lagoon ran red and dead fish washed ashore, the community did not stay silent, as it might have under the dictatorship it had lately shed. A local scientist tested the water and documented the pollution; the community's own environmental group gathered the evidence; the national environment agency was pressed into ordering the factory to stop; the community took the company to court; and the villagers protested in the streets. The factory was not shut down, and the government, hungry for foreign investment, sided against the community more than once. But the community won real things: the pollution documented and named, the regulator forced to act, national and international attention drawn, a precedent-setting case brought, and — amid the wider wave of protest — the temporary closure of two other fishmeal factories nearby. The struggle continues. But a village that would once have been silenced made itself heard, and made a foreign polluter answer. It is worth setting down what that community actually did, because it shows both the power and the limits of a village standing up in a young democracy. The harm was concrete and provable: within months of the factory opening, the lagoon that the community had tended as a reserve for over twenty years turned an alarming colour, dead fish and marine animals washed up, and swimmers complained of skin problems. Rather than merely protest, a local scientist tested the water and found it poisoned, and the community’s own environmental group built a documented record. That evidence was the lever: it forced the national environment agency to order the factory to treat its waste, it grounded a lawsuit, and it gave the national and international press a story it could not ignore. The community did not win everything — the government intervened for the investor and the factory kept running — but it converted private suffering into public, documented accountability, which under the old dictatorship would have been unthinkable.

The Gambian story teaches two things about stopping destructive projects here — including its honest limits, which this guide states plainly and up front, because they are large. The first is the pattern that runs through every guide: not a single silver bullet, but the combination of every lever — the affected community, the documentation, the coalition, and the pressure on the deciders — pressed together. The second is distinctly Gambian, and it forced a foreign polluter to answer. **A community — especially one that manages its own reserve and can test and document the harm — can use community science, litigation, protest, and the new freedom of a young democracy to force accountability from a foreign polluter and its regulators, drawing national and international attention and winning real, if partial, protection for its environment and its livelihood.** That power — the community reserve, the monitoring, the litigation, the protest, and the space of a new democracy — is The Gambia's distinctive lever, and this guide is built around it. What makes this lever distinctive, and worth understanding precisely, is that it does not depend on a friendly government — which a Gambian community fighting a wanted investor rarely has — but on evidence and voice, which a community can supply itself. Hard, tested proof of harm is difficult for even an investor-friendly government to ignore entirely, because it can be brought to the regulator, the courts, the press, and the international community all at once, and because it converts a dispute the government would call mere complaint into a documented breach of the country’s own rules. And the new democratic freedom, hard-won after decades of dictatorship, gives a community the ability to organise, protest, and publish that it never had before. The community’s task is therefore to make the harm undeniable and to use its new voice fully and lawfully, so that accountability becomes unavoidable even where a full stop is not — which is what the Gunjur community achieved.

But the same story carries honest warnings, and this guide will not hide them; it places them first and takes them seriously. The Gunjur community did **not** shut the factory down; the government, eager for foreign investment, **sided against the community** — even having the regulator's case settled out of court — and the community's own lawsuit **stalled for years**. Protesters were **arrested and charged**, so the work carried real legal risk. And the win was **partial and the struggle ongoing** — accountability and attention, not a project stopped. This guide is written with both truths in view: the real, distinctive power of community science, litigation, and protest in a new democracy — and the government's investment bias, the stalled courts, the legal risk, and the partial, ongoing nature of the win, which qualify it heavily. These qualifications are not footnotes; they are the core of an honest picture, and a community should weigh them hard before it begins. The government’s investment bias is the largest: a poor country hungry for foreign money has strong reasons to favour an investor over a community, and in Gunjur the government went so far as to have its own regulator’s case against the polluter settled out of court. The stalled courts are real: the community’s own lawsuit has languished for years with adjournment after adjournment, so litigation here is a slow marker laid down, not a quick remedy. The legal risk is real: protesters were arrested and charged, and a community must plan for its people’s safety and stay within the law. And the partial, ongoing nature of the win is the honest heart of it: the factory kept operating, and the struggle continues — what was won was accountability, attention, and partial protection, which are real and worth having, but are not a project stopped. If your fight, too, is against a foreign polluter your government wants, and you can document the harm and raise your voice, this is the guide for it — clear-eyed about how hard it is.

Most communities do not know HOW to make that power effective. They watch the water foul and the fish die and assume "the government wants the factory and nothing can be done," and never test the water, never document the harm, never bring the case. They generate private suffering without organised evidence. The gap between accountability won and harm ignored is rarely apathy — Gunjur proved a village will act. It is method: knowing how to monitor and document, litigate and protest, and use the new freedom to force an answer.

## The Strategic Framework

Effective campaigns follow the same underlying pattern, whether the goal is to stop a polluting factory, a harmful development, or another destructive project:

```
STEP 1: TARGET IDENTIFICATION  (find the project, the harm, and the deciders)
        |
STEP 2: DOCUMENTATION          (build the evidence and the community science)
        |
STEP 3: LOCAL OPPOSITION        (build the community and the coalition)
        |
STEP 4: LEGAL & REGULATORY     (the environment agency, the courts, the reserve)
        |
STEP 5: MEDIA STRATEGY         (make it a national and international cause)
```

These steps operate **simultaneously, not in sequence**. The community science feeds the litigation and the media at once; the coalition grows; a regulator's order or an international story is both pressure and a spur to the next. The community that runs all five together, each reinforcing the others, is the one that wins what can be won — accountability forced, harm named, a reserve defended. The Gunjur community did exactly this — it monitored, documented, litigated, and protested together.

## The Levers That Are Distinctly Strong Here

Every country's system has its own pressure points. The Gambia's give communities a distinctive set:

- **The community reserve and community science.** A **community-managed reserve** and the ability to **test and document harm** give a community standing and hard evidence, as Gunjur's reserve and water-testing did. This is the lever that most distinguishes a Gambian fight of this kind, and it rewards close attention. A community-managed reserve is more than a conservation area: it is a claim to stewardship, a demonstration that the community has cared for this place and has standing to defend it, and a focus that turns a diffuse worry about pollution into a defence of a specific, valued, recognised place. And community science — even something as basic as a documented record and a water test done with a scientist’s help — is what converts a community’s certainty that it is being harmed into proof a regulator must act on and a court can weigh. Together, a reserve to defend and the science to prove the harm to it give a community both the standing and the evidence that a fight against an investor-backed polluter most requires, as they did in Gunjur.
- **The new democratic space.** The **new freedom** of a young democracy lets communities speak, protest, and organise as they once could not, as Gunjur's people found.
- **The litigation and the regulator.** The **courts** and the **environment agency** can be pressed to hold a polluter to account, as they were against the factory.
- **The livelihood and food-security stakes.** The threat to **fish, food, and livelihood** unites a community powerfully, as it did in Gunjur.
- **The international attention.** A story of a **foreign polluter and a fishing village** draws national and international attention and allies, as Gunjur's did.

## What This Guide Will Not Pretend

It would be dishonest to promise these levers deliver a clean win or a project stopped. The Gunjur factory kept operating; the government **favoured the investment**; the courts were **slow**; and protesters faced **arrest**. This guide takes those limits seriously — it states them first and throughout — and shows you how to use the real, distinctive tools The Gambia offers — the community reserve, the community science, the litigation, the protest, and the new democratic space — as Gunjur used them to force a foreign polluter to answer, even where a full stop was out of reach.

## The Levels of Decision

A Gambian development runs through several tracks at once, and each is a place to intervene.

**The government.** The **government** grants contracts and licences and — honestly — often **favours foreign investment**, even against a community, as it favoured the fishmeal factories; so it is a decider to be pressured and moved, not relied upon. It is worth being clear-eyed about this from the start, because it shapes the whole strategy. In many countries a community’s first hope is that the government will enforce its own rules; in The Gambia, a community fighting a wanted foreign investor must often assume the opposite — that the government will lean toward the investment — and build a campaign that can force accountability despite that lean, rather than relying on the government’s goodwill. This is why the Gambian strategy leans so heavily on the levers a community controls itself, its science and its voice, and on the external pressure of the courts, the press, and the international community, which can move a government that its own citizens cannot. Treating the government as a force to be pressured, not a protector to be petitioned, is the realistic starting point here.

**The environment agency.** The **National Environment Agency** and the environmental rules can be **pressed to act** against a polluter, as the agency was pressed to order the factory to stop.

**The courts.** The **courts** can be used to hold a polluter to account and set a precedent, though — honestly — they are **slow**, as the Gunjur case has been.

**The community and the reserve.** The **community**, its **environmental group**, and its **community-managed reserve** are the force that documents the harm and makes the case, as Gunjur's did.

**The public and the world.** The **new democratic freedom**, the **national press**, and the **international attention** are levers that a community can now use as it once could not.

The distinctive levers are the **community reserve and science**, the **new democratic space**, the **litigation and regulator**, and the **livelihood stakes**. Find out exactly where the project stands, and, crucially, **what harm you can document**, **how the reserve and the rules can be used**, and **how the public and the world can be reached**, because those are often where a project can be held to account.

## Follow the Project, the Harm, and the Deciders

Behind a project stand a proponent — often a foreign company — and the government that licenses it. Trace who decides, who profits, and — importantly here — **what harm the project does to the water, the fish, and the community**, **what community science can document it**, **how the reserve and the environmental rules apply**, and **how the new freedom and the world's attention can be used**. Two questions unlock most Gambian fights of this kind: **What harm can the community document with its own science?** and **How can the regulator, the courts, and the public be pressed — in the new democratic space — to force accountability?** The community science and the new freedom are, together, the heart of most such campaigns. These two work together in a way particular to the fight here: the community science supplies the undeniable proof of harm, and the new democratic freedom supplies the ability to act on it — to organise, protest, litigate, and publish — that a community under the old dictatorship simply did not have. A campaign with the science but no freedom to use it would be silenced; one with the freedom but no hard evidence would be dismissed as mere grievance by a government that wants the investment. Joined together — tested, documented harm carried loudly and lawfully by a newly free community to the regulator, the courts, and the world — they make accountability difficult to refuse, even for a government inclined to shield the polluter. That is the combination the Gunjur community assembled, and it is why a foreign polluter was made to answer.

# STEP 1: TARGET IDENTIFICATION

Every effective campaign begins by finding the exact project and harm that govern the threat — and the leverage the community holds, which here is often the documented harm and the new freedom. Vague opposition goes nowhere; a fight aimed at "the factory, whose waste we can test and prove is poisoning our reserve and our fish, which we can take to the regulator and the courts and the public in our new freedom" is a campaign — the kind that forced accountability in Gunjur. Answer five questions.

## The Five Core Questions

**1. What harm can the community document?** This is the first Gambian question of this kind. What **harm to the water, the fish, and the community** can be **tested and documented** with community science, as Gunjur tested the lagoon. Assess this honestly and precisely at the outset, because in The Gambia the hardness of the science is what most determines how much a campaign can force. A vague sense of harm, however genuine, gives an investor-friendly government and its regulator every excuse to do nothing. But documented, tested proof — water samples, measured contamination, a recorded pattern of dead fish and fouled water — is a fact that must be weighed, and that the courts, the press, and the international community can rely on. A community should therefore establish, first, what harm can actually be tested and proven and who can help prove it, for the strength of that evidence is the foundation on which the regulator, the courts, the reserve claim, and every other lever depends.

**2. How do the reserve and the rules apply?** Does a **community-managed reserve** or an **environmental rule** give standing and a legal handle, as the Bolong Fenyo reserve did. This question rewards early attention, because a stewardship claim can be the difference between being heard and being brushed aside. Where a community formally manages a reserve, it has a recognised interest and a record of care that strengthen its standing before the regulator and the courts and its sympathy before the public, and it gives the campaign a concrete place to defend rather than an abstract grievance. Even where no formal reserve exists, establishing the community’s long stewardship of the affected place — its fishery, its water, its coast — can serve a similar purpose. A community should therefore work out early what stewardship claim it can make, for it shapes both its standing and the story it tells.

**3. How can the new freedom be used?** How can the community **organise, protest, and speak** in the new democratic space, as Gunjur's people did.

**4. Who can be pressed — regulator, courts, public?** Which of the **environment agency, the courts, and the public** can be pressed to force accountability, as they were against the factory.

**5. Who decides, who profits, and what is at stake?** Which company and officials are behind it, who profits — and honestly, whether the government backs it; and what the project does to the water, the fish, and the livelihood.

## A Worked Example

Suppose a foreign-owned facility pollutes the water, harming a reserve, the fish, and the community's livelihood.

Working the five questions: **what harm can be documented**? **How do the reserve and rules apply**? **How can the new freedom be used**? **Who can be pressed**? And the deciders are the government and the company, with the community science, the regulator, the courts, and the public as the pressure points — while honestly weighing the government's likely bias toward the investment.

Within a season you have moved from "the government wants the factory and nothing can be done" to a target list: document the harm; use the reserve and the freedom; press the regulator and the courts; unite around livelihood; draw attention. That is a campaign, not a complaint — and it draws directly on how Gunjur forced a foreign polluter to answer. Notice what the exercise did: it turned a sense that the government wants the factory and nothing can be done into a set of concrete, workable tasks — document the harm, use the reserve and the freedom, press the regulator and the courts, unite around livelihood, draw attention — each tied to a specific lever that The Gambia genuinely provides, and each honest about its limits. That reframing, from resignation before an investor-friendly government to a plan built on the community’s own science and voice, is the difference the questions make, and the first — what harm can the community document — often decides how much accountability the campaign can force.

# STEP 2: DOCUMENTATION — HOW TO BUILD AN UNASSAILABLE CASE

Documentation is the foundation everything else stands on — and here, where the government may favour the polluter, hard evidence is more important still. The regulator, a court, a journalist, and the community all move on evidence, not indignation — and in The Gambia, the community science that proves the harm is precisely what a campaign turns into accountability, so document it well and early. Build your file in three layers.

## Layer 1: The Project, the Contract, and the Deciders

Get the facts the case turns on. Identify the **project, its owner, its contract, and its licence**; and establish **which regulator and rules** apply and **whether a reserve** is affected, as the Gunjur factory operated beside a community reserve under a contract from the old regime. Establish the deciders and the project precisely — including, honestly, whether the government backs it — because they are what the evidence must move.

## Layer 2: The Harm — the Community Science

Document the reality the polluter denies, and here the **community science is central**. Record, and where you can **test**, the **harm to the water, the fish, and the community** — the pollution, the dead fish, the fouled reserve, the health effects, the lost catch — as a local scientist tested the Gunjur lagoon and found it poisoned. Record the **value** of what is harmed — the reserve, the fishery, the livelihood, the food security. Photographs, samples, tests, and testimony make the harm undeniable. This is the evidence that forces the regulator and moves the court and the public. The community science, in particular, deserves to be gathered with care, because it is the foundation the whole campaign stands on and the one thing an investor-friendly government finds hardest to wave away. A vague complaint of pollution is easy to dismiss; a documented, tested record — water samples showing the contamination, photographs of the dead fish and the discoloured lagoon, a log of the harm over time — is a fact the regulator is bound to weigh and the court and the press can rely on. Even modest science, done with the help of a sympathetic scientist or an allied laboratory, can be decisive, as a single scientist’s testing of the Gunjur lagoon anchored the whole case. A community should therefore invest early in making its evidence hard, specific, and undeniable, for the strength of that evidence is what determines how much accountability it can force.

## Layer 3: The Evidence, the Standing, and the Remedy

Finally, document the case. Does the **community science** prove the harm? Do the **reserve and the rules** give standing and a remedy? And can the **regulator, the courts, and the public** be moved? Each is a separate ground — for the agency, the court, the media, and the community — and together they make a case that is hard to wave away, even by a government that favours the investment. This is the layer that most often decides Gambian fights of this kind, because here harm is proven by the community's own science, standing is established through the reserve and the rules, and a remedy is pressed on the regulator and the courts — exactly the case that forced accountability in Gunjur, where a tested and documented harm, a community reserve, and an organised, newly-free community together made a foreign polluter answer. Assembling all three grounds together — the proven harm, the standing, and the remedy — is what gives a Gambian case its force, because each does a different job and they reinforce one another: the science supplies the undeniable harm, the reserve and the rules supply the standing to demand action, and the regulator and the courts supply the remedy that can be pressed. A campaign that proves harm but has no standing to demand a remedy, or that has standing but no hard proof, gives an investor-friendly government room to do nothing. Shown together — a tested, documented harm, a community with a recognised claim to protect the place, and a specific remedy the regulator and courts can grant — they make accountability far harder to refuse, which is how a foreign polluter was made to answer in Gunjur even without being shut down.

# WHAT TO GATHER, AND WHERE TO FIND IT

This is the practical companion to Step 2: the specific records that decide Gambian cases of this kind, and where to obtain them. You will not need every item; you will need the few that prove the harm, establish the standing, and press the remedy.

## The Project, the Harm, and the Paper Trail

- **The project and contract** — what operates, by whom, under what contract and licence.
- **The regulator and rules** — the environment agency's powers and the rules that apply.
- **The community science** — tests, samples, photographs, and records of the harm.
- **The reserve and standing** — the community-managed reserve and the rights it gives.
- **The livelihood harm** — the lost fish, the fouled water, the health effects.

## How to Get Them

The project and contract are matters of record, though — honestly — sometimes hard to obtain where a government favours an investor, so gather what you can and note what is withheld. The **community science** is the heart of the case, and much of it the community can produce itself — photographs, records, and, crucially, **water and environmental tests**, done with the help of a scientist or an allied laboratory, as a local scientist's testing anchored the Gunjur case. The **reserve and the rules** are matters of national and community record that establish standing, as the Bolong Fenyo reserve did. Lean on **allies**: the community's environmental group, sympathetic scientists, national and international environmental organisations, and a free-er press. Move promptly, and — given the legal risk protesters have faced — take care.

## The Ground Evidence

- **The proven harm** — the tests, samples, and records of pollution and its effects.
- **The reserve and standing** — the community-managed reserve and the rights it confers.
- **The livelihood stakes** — the lost catch, the threatened food, the health effects.
- **The regulator's duty** — the rules the environment agency is bound to enforce.
- **The attention** — the national and international interest a foreign-polluter story draws.

## Organise It So It Persuades

Keep a **master timeline** of the project, the harm, the tests, and every step of the fight — the spine of any complaint to the regulator, case in court, or story in the press. Keep a **document index** so any fact can be sourced fast. And keep an **argument bank** (developed in a later section) that pairs each fact with what it proves, to whom. A well-ordered file is not bureaucracy; it is what lets a community member, a regulator, a judge, a journalist, and an ally all see the same clear picture fast, and it guards against the campaign's own worst enemy, a claim that cannot be sourced when the company or a friendly government pushes back. In The Gambia, where the deciders may favour the polluter, the campaign with the harder, better-documented science is very often the one that forces at least accountability, as Gunjur's did.

# STEP 3: BUILDING LOCAL OPPOSITION — HOW TO BUILD THE COMMUNITY AND THE COALITION

Evidence does not win on its own. A united community and a broad coalition do — and in The Gambia, the Gunjur community proves that a village, newly free to organise, that documents the harm and stands together can force a foreign polluter to answer. A campaign's strength here is that unity and that new freedom: a community that speaks with one voice, in a democracy that now lets it.

## Phase 1: The Core and the Community Science

Start with a committed core rooted in the **affected community and its environmental group**, and do the distinctively Gambian first work: **document and test the harm**, so the pollution is proven and undeniable, as Gunjur's people tested the lagoon. Assign roles — the community science, the litigation, the regulator complaints, the protest and media, and the reserve. Agree on the goal — accountability, remediation, and protection, honestly aimed — and on the risks, for protest here has carried arrest. Draw on the community's stake in its fishery and its reserve.

## Phase 2: The Coalition and the New Freedom

Then widen deliberately, and use the **new democratic freedom** that is The Gambia's distinctive opening, as Gunjur's people organised and protested as they could not have under the old regime. Bring in the **whole fishing community and its associations**, whose unity around livelihood is powerful; the **community's environmental group** and **sympathetic scientists**, who prove the harm; **national and international environmental organisations**, whose support and attention amplify the case; sympathetic voices in **the press and public life**, now freer; and the **wider affected communities**, for as in Gunjur the harm often spreads along the coast. The Gunjur campaign drew its power from exactly this breadth — the community, the science, the new freedom, the allies — which is precisely the spread a Gambian campaign of this kind needs. The reach to national and international environmental organisations deserves particular emphasis, because in a fight against a foreign polluter a government wants, outside attention is often what tips the balance. A small fishing community rarely has, on its own, the means to conduct rigorous science, to reach the world’s press, or to make an investor and a government feel reputational cost abroad — and these are exactly the capabilities that carried the Gunjur case beyond the village. Establishing early which environmental organisations and journalists work on fisheries, pollution, and foreign investment, and can supply that science and amplification, therefore shapes what the whole campaign can attempt. Outside attention also offers a measure of protection to local campaigners, whose exposure to arrest is real, by raising the cost of silencing them.

**A worked coalition example.** Facing a polluting factory, the fishing community and its environmental group document and test the harm; scientists and national and international organisations amplify it; and the community protests and litigates in its new freedom. No single group could force accountability from a foreign polluter the government wants; together they cover the community, the science, and the attention — the model Gunjur built. The lesson of that model is that no single part of a coalition needs to be able to win alone; what matters is that, between them, the parts cover every front a Gambian fight of this kind requires — the community to stand and voice the grievance, the scientists to prove the harm, the environmental organisations to amplify and protect, and the press to reach a government that outside pressure moves. A community that maps these fronts and finds a partner for each builds something far stronger than the sum of its parts, and far harder for an investor-friendly government to ignore than any one group acting alone.

## Phase 3: Sustaining It

A campaign can run for years — the Gunjur struggle still goes on — so endurance matters, and honesty about the long road. Meet on a rhythm that can be kept. Renew the coalition, bringing in new people as others tire. Give people concrete tasks — the science, the complaints, the litigation, the protest, the media. Mark the wins that come — a test completed, a regulator's order, a story published, a factory temporarily closed, a precedent set — because in a long and partial struggle morale is sustained by real gains, however incomplete. Keep the coalition broad, and guard against the **government's investment bias**, the **slowness of the courts**, the **length of the fight**, and — importantly — the **legal risk**, for Gunjur's protesters faced arrest. Protect your people, keep within the law, and value partial gains: accountability forced and harm named are real, even short of a stop. Track and name these gains deliberately, because a campaign that may run for years against a government that favours the polluter will exhaust a community if success is measured only by the factory’s closure. A water test completed, a regulator’s order issued, a story published at home or abroad, a factory temporarily closed, a precedent laid down, an official forced to respond — each is real progress and a sign the pressure is working, and each is a reason for the community to sustain the effort and the risk. A campaign that marks its interim gains keeps its people through a long, hard struggle; one that waits silently for a stop that may never come too often loses heart and dissolves before it has won even the accountability that was within reach.

## Phase 4: The Community, the Science, and the Freedom

Here is The Gambia's distinctive strength, on two levels. First, the **community and the science**: unite the fishing community and arm it with **tested, documented harm**, so the case is both broad and undeniable, as it was in Gunjur. Second, and distinctively, the **new freedom and the wider world**: use the **new democratic space** to organise, protest, and litigate, and draw **national and international attention**, so a community once silenced is now heard, as Gunjur was heard. Keep it **united, evidence-based, and lawfully bold**, because in The Gambia it is a community that documents the harm, stands together, and uses its hard-won freedom that forces a foreign polluter to answer — as the Gunjur community did, even where the government wished otherwise.

# STEP 4: LEGAL & REGULATORY CHALLENGES — THE ENVIRONMENT AGENCY, THE COURTS, AND THE COMMUNITY RESERVE

The Gambia's environment agency, its courts, its environmental rules, and — distinctively — its community-managed reserves are a real toolkit, though an imperfect one you must use with clear eyes. You do not need a clean court victory to win: a regulator's order, a factory temporarily closed, a precedent set, harm officially named — each is a real gain, and together they can force accountability. Engage lawyers and allies early — but understand both the tools and their limits.

## The Environment Agency and the Rules

The first lever is the **National Environment Agency and the environmental rules**. The agency can be **pressed to order a polluter to stop, treat its waste, or remediate**, as it ordered the Gunjur factory. It may be slow or subject to pressure — honestly, the government has intervened for investors — but a documented harm and a persistent community can force it to act. Bring the community science to the agency and hold it to its duty.

## The Courts and the Precedent

The second lever is the **courts and the precedent**. A community can **sue a polluter**, and even a slow or stalled case — as Gunjur's has been — can **set a precedent, apply pressure, and keep the harm before the public**, so that others may follow. Honestly, the courts here are slow and the road long; but a case brought is a marker laid down and a pressure sustained. Litigate with patience and with allies who know the courts. It helps to be realistic about what litigation here does and does not do. A case against a well-connected polluter may take years, may be adjourned again and again, and may never reach a clean verdict — the Gunjur community’s suit has languished in exactly this way. But a case brought is not wasted: it lays down a public marker that the harm is contested, it keeps the issue before the courts and the press, it can deter a polluter wary of an adverse ruling, and it may set a precedent that later communities can build on. Litigation is best understood here not as the decisive blow but as one sustained source of pressure among several, valuable for the record and the precedent it creates as much as for any judgment it might eventually win.

## The Community Reserve and the Standing

The third and distinctive lever is the **community-managed reserve and the standing it gives**. A **reserve the community itself manages** — as Gunjur managed Bolong Fenyo for over two decades — grounds the community's right to protect the place and strengthens its standing before the regulator and the courts. Use the reserve: it makes the community not a bystander but a recognised steward with a claim. It is worth dwelling on why a community-managed reserve is so valuable in a fight like this. Standing — the recognised right to bring a complaint or a case — can be a real obstacle for ordinary residents objecting to a development, but a community that formally manages a reserve has an evident, defensible interest in protecting it, and a record of stewardship that strengthens its voice before the regulator, the courts, and the public. The reserve also gives the campaign a concrete, sympathetic focus: not pollution in the abstract, but the poisoning of a specific place the community has cared for and the nation has recognised. A community that holds or can establish such a stewardship claim should build its campaign around it, as the Gunjur community built theirs around the reserve they had tended for over two decades.

## Where to Turn and With Whom

The regulator complaint goes through the **National Environment Agency**; the litigation through the **courts** and lawyers; the standing through the **community reserve and the rules**; the pressure through **protest, the press, and international allies**. Get help early: environmental lawyers, sympathetic scientists, and national and international organisations know the agency, the courts, and the science, and they will support a community with a hard, well-documented case, as they supported Gunjur. Help engaged early is worth far more than help sought after, and in The Gambia the strongest strategy marries the **community and its tested science** to the **regulator, the courts, the reserve, and the new freedom**, so that a foreign polluter the government wants is still met by an organised, evidence-armed community pressing every lever — as the Gunjur community met the factory, and forced it to answer even without shutting it down.

# TURNING YOUR EVIDENCE INTO ARGUMENTS

You have a file of evidence. Now you must turn it into arguments — because the same fact persuades the regulator, a court, a journalist, an ally, and the community in different ways. Learning to translate your evidence for each audience is what lets a community reach every lever at once.

## One Fact, Many Arguments

Take a single documented fact — say, that tests show a factory's waste has poisoned a community reserve and killed the fish a village depends on. That one fact becomes:

- **A regulatory argument:** the polluter is in breach of the rules the agency must enforce — the case for an order to stop or remediate.
- **A legal argument:** the harm and the breach ground a case and a precedent — the case for the courts.
- **A standing argument:** this is our community-managed reserve, and we have the right to protect it — the case for our voice.
- **A livelihood argument:** this poisons the fish and the food our community lives on — the case that unites everyone.
- **An international argument:** a foreign company is polluting a poor community's coast — the case that draws attention and allies.

Same fact, five arguments, five audiences. Go through your strongest facts and, for each, ask what it proves to each audience. That table is your campaign's argument bank — and in The Gambia the arguments that most often force at least accountability are the **regulatory** and **community-science** ones, because they make the harm undeniable, and the **international** one, because attention can move a government that its own community cannot. Keep the arguments anchored to the same documented facts, so a claim made to the regulator can be backed and a claim made to a court can be proven; consistency across audiences is itself persuasive. And be honest about the state of the fight: acknowledge the government's bias and the partial nature of the likely win, and argue for the accountability and protection that can be won.

## Match the Argument to the Audience

**The environment agency** responds to a documented breach of the rules it is bound to enforce, pressed persistently.

**The courts** want the harm and the breach framed precisely, with the tests and the records.

**The government** responds, where it can be moved at all, to national and international pressure and reputational cost.

**Journalists** need a specific, vivid story with a hook: a fishing village and a poisoned lagoon against a foreign polluter.

**The community** needs the argument clear and close to home: our fish, our food, our reserve, our health. The livelihood argument, though it moves people most, must stay anchored to the documented harm if it is to serve the other fronts as well. The dependence of a fishing community on its catch and its clean water rallies people rightly; but the same case, stated precisely — the measured contamination, the specific breach, the concrete remedy — is also what the regulator, the court, and the international ally require. A campaign that lets the livelihood argument drift into exaggeration hands the company and the government an easy rebuttal and weakens the case built on the same ground. Keep the livelihood argument vivid but scrupulously true, so that the fear that stirs the community and the evidence that moves the regulator are one and the same, drawn from a single reliable record.

**The international world** responds to a foreign company polluting a poor community and to a David-and-Goliath fight. Because this audience is moved by exactly such stakes, a campaign gains by framing its case in those terms. The international community — the environmental organisations, the outlets that cover foreign investment and pollution, the sympathetic public abroad — responds not only to the harm to a single lagoon but to the pattern of a powerful foreign company polluting a poor community’s coast while a cash-hungry government looks away, and to the courage of a small community fighting back. A campaign that can show its fight is both emblematic and winnable of at least accountability speaks directly to what this community responds to, and can draw the science, the amplification, and the protective attention that a small village cannot generate alone — the very support that carried the Gunjur case to the world.

# STEP 5: MEDIA STRATEGY — HOW COVERAGE SHIFTS OUTCOMES

Media is not vanity; it is a lever — and in The Gambia, where a government may favour a polluter its own community opposes, national and international attention is one of the few forces that can move it, so coverage is among the most powerful levers of all. Coverage unites the community, presses the regulator, shames the polluter, and — crucially — reaches a government that responds to reputational cost and international eyes. The Gunjur struggle became a national and international story — the poisoned lagoon, the dead fish, the fishing village, the foreign factory.

## Build the Story

Reporters and the public need specifics, and here they need the harm and the David-and-Goliath stakes. "A factory is controversial" is not a story; "a foreign factory has turned our community's protected lagoon red and killed the fish our village lives on, and we tested the water to prove it" is — the kind of story that carried Gunjur to the world. Lead with your strongest verified facts — the tests, the dead fish, the reserve, the lost catch — and offer a human voice and the stakes: a fisher, a scientist, an image of the fouled lagoon. In The Gambia the story of a fishing village standing up to a foreign polluter is one the nation and the world respond to.

## Reach the Right Outlets

Work outward in rings. **The community and local voices** first. Then the **national outlets**, now freer, because a poisoned coast and a threatened fishery are national concerns. Then, distinctively and importantly, the **international ring** — the world's press, the environmental organisations, the outlets that cover foreign investment and pollution — because international attention reaches a government that its own community may not move, and draws powerful allies. Feed each ring what it needs, and let coverage in one pull in the next. The **international ring is distinctly important** here, because it can move a government that favours the investment.

## Sustain It, and Keep It True

A single article changes little; a drumbeat over years changes decisions. Plan a sequence — the harm, the tests, the regulator, the case, the protest, the closures — so there is always a next hook, and build your own clear record so the story endures. Keep messaging disciplined, accurate, and honest; the company and the government will seize on any exaggeration, and a credible, evidence-and-livelihood-centred campaign is what draws sustained national and international attention. Verified, specific, science-grounded, and livelihood-focused — that is the coverage that shifts outcomes here, and that carried Gunjur's case to the world.

# EMAILS & LETTERS

Written communications are how you complain, request, litigate, create a record, and reach the bodies that can act — the regulator, the courts, the government, the allies, and the press. Each does double duty: it asks for something and it documents that you asked. Keep copies of everything, and send important items so that receipt is provable. Below are adaptable templates for the situations you will meet most often. Keep them factual, specific, and grounded in the tested harm, the standing, and the remedy.

## 8A. Complaint to the Environment Agency

**To:** the National Environment Agency
**Subject:** Complaint of pollution by [company] at [place]

We, [community/organisation], report that [company] is polluting [our reserve/coast] at [place], as our tests and records show: [harm]. We ask the Agency to enforce the rules — to order [the polluter] to stop, treat its waste, and remediate — as it is bound to do.

## 8B. Request for Information

**To:** the relevant government authority
**Subject:** Request for information on [project]

We, [community/organisation], request the **contract, the licence, and the environmental terms** for [project] at [place], granted to [company]. As the affected community, we are entitled to the record. Please provide the documents.

## 8C. Notice of Legal Action

**To:** [company] / the relevant authority
**Subject:** Notice regarding pollution at [place]

We, [community/organisation], have documented and tested the harm [company] is causing at [place], in breach of the environmental rules and our rights over our community reserve. We call on you to cease and remediate, and we reserve our right to bring the matter before the courts.

## 8D. Approach to Scientists & Environmental Organisations

**To:** [scientist / environmental organisation]
**Subject:** Help us prove and fight the harm at [place]

We are opposing [company]'s pollution of [our reserve and fishery] at [place]. We seek your help to **test and document the harm** to scientific standard and to amplify our case, as such support anchored and carried the Gunjur community's fight.

## 8E. Appeal to the Community & Fishers

**To:** the [fishing community / associations]
**Subject:** Defend our fishery and our reserve from [company]

We write to all who depend on [our waters]. [Company]'s pollution threatens our fish, our food, and our reserve. We ask you to stand together — lawfully and united — to document the harm and demand accountability, as the fishing community of Gunjur did.

## 8F. Appeal to the Government

**To:** the government / relevant ministry
**Subject:** [Company] harms our community and our coast

We, [community/organisation], inform the government that [company]'s pollution harms our reserve, our fishery, and our people, as our evidence shows. We ask the government to enforce its own rules and protect its citizens over an investor's convenience, and we are bringing our case to the nation and the world.

## 8G. Email to a Journalist

**To:** [journalist]
**Subject:** Story tip: a fishing village vs a foreign polluter — [specific finding]

I follow your coverage of [beat]. We have a national — and international — story, with a clear hook: [tests] show [finding — e.g. "a foreign factory has poisoned our community's protected lagoon and killed our fish"]. It echoes the Gunjur fishmeal fight. I can share our evidence and connect you with a fisher and a scientist. Could we talk this week?

## 8H. Public Statement

**To:** the public and the authorities
**Subject:** [Coalition] will defend our coast from [company]

We, [coalition], will not see [company] poison our reserve, our fishery, and our health. As the Gunjur community tested the water, took its case to the regulator and the courts, and made a foreign polluter answer, we will document the harm, use our freedom to stand together lawfully, and demand accountability. Our coast and our fish are not for sale.

## Using the Templates Well

Send important communications so that receipt is provable, and log every one in your timeline — the record of a complaint, a notice, or a request is itself evidence, and matters more where a government may favour the polluter. Keep them factual, specific, and grounded in the tested harm, the standing, and the remedy; attach your evidence; and route them, where you can, through the community, the scientists, the allies, and the press. The reply, or the silence, becomes part of your record, and in The Gambia that record feeds the levers that matter most: the community science, the new democratic space, the litigation and regulator, and the livelihood stakes that made Gunjur's foreign polluter answer.

# IF YOU HAVE LITTLE TIME OR FEW RESOURCES: THE RAPID-FIRE VERSION

Not everyone can run a years-long campaign. With a few hours a week you can still do real work to defend your coast and fishery. In rough order of impact for the effort:

**1. Document and test the harm.** Record the pollution and, if you can, test the water (Step 2, 8D).

**2. Complain to the environment agency.** Report the breach and demand enforcement (8A).

**3. Unite the fishing community.** Rally around the shared threat to fish and food (Step 3, 8E).

**4. Draw attention — national and international.** Take the David-and-Goliath story out (8G).

**5. Use the reserve and your standing.** Assert your right to protect your community reserve (Step 4).

**6. Consider litigation with allies.** Bring or support a case, knowing it is slow (8C).

**7. Persist, protect your people, and value partial wins.** Accountability forced is real; stay within the law.

Do only the first three and you will have documented the harm, pressed the regulator, and united the community — the foundation of a Gambian campaign. Then come back and read the rest. Even the shortest version has a spine: document the harm, press the regulator, unite the community. A note for the resource-poor community in particular: in The Gambia the decisive early moves are mostly within reach, though one requires help. Uniting the fishing community around its threatened livelihood, complaining to the environment agency, and asserting a stewardship claim cost mainly organising and courage, not money; and even the community science, the one real expense, can often be modest — a documented record, photographs, a single water test done with a sympathetic scientist or an allied laboratory, as one scientist’s testing anchored the Gunjur case. What the fight demands above all is not resources but hard evidence, unity, and the lawful courage to use a new freedom. A community that documents the harm, presses the regulator, and unites its people has already assembled the core of what made a foreign polluter answer in Gunjur — while holding honestly to the knowledge that the win is likely partial and the road long.

# WHEN THE SYSTEM IS TILTED

The Gambia's system, with a young democracy, a free-er press, environmental rules, community-managed reserves, and a place in an international community that watches how foreign investment behaves, offers a community real tools to defend its coast — but it is tilted in specific and serious ways you must understand, so you neither waste effort nor grow discouraged.

## Where the Tilt Shows

The tilt here is real and large, and this guide will not soften it. The government, eager for **foreign investment**, has **sided against communities** — even having a regulator's case against a polluter settled out of court. The **courts are slow**, and cases stall for years. **Protest has carried arrest**, so speaking out has a real cost. And the win is usually **partial** — accountability, attention, a precedent, a temporary closure — not a polluter shut down. Where these tilts bite, a campaign that assumes the government will enforce its own rules against an investor, or that a clean stop is near, will be disappointed — which is precisely why the distinctive strategy is to arm the community with undeniable science, use the new freedom and the courts for all they are worth, and draw the international attention that can move a government its own people cannot.

## What Still Works — And Some Is Real

The decisive point is that even against this serious tilt, The Gambia's levers are real, and some are strong.

**Community science still forces the issue.** A water test and a documented record make harm undeniable, as they did in Gunjur.

**The new freedom still lets communities act.** A young democracy gives voice and the right to protest where a dictatorship silenced them.

**The regulator and courts can still be pressed.** Slowly and imperfectly, they can be moved to orders and precedents.

**A community reserve still gives standing.** A community-managed reserve grounds the right to protect the place.

**International attention still moves a government.** Reputational cost and the world's eyes can shift what a community alone cannot.

## The Honest Frame

So the honest frame for The Gambia is realistic, sober, and clear-eyed: the government often favours the investment, the courts are slow, protest carries risk, and the win is usually partial — but community science, the new freedom, the litigation and regulator, and international attention give a community real levers, and forcing accountability, naming the harm, and defending a reserve are real, if partial, victories. So diagnose your fight honestly: **document the harm; use the reserve and the freedom; press the regulator and the courts; unite around livelihood; draw attention — and protect your people, and keep within the law.** Do not assume a foreign polluter cannot be made to answer; the Gunjur community made one answer, though it kept operating.

# WHEN IT'S ACTUAL CORRUPTION — AND WHO IS CAPTURED

The Gambia is a nation lately freed from a corrupt dictatorship, and concerns about foreign money, opaque contracts, and officials favouring investors over communities are real and present, so this section matters. When a project is driven not by the public interest but by an investor's money and a government's hunger for it, and advanced despite clear harm, your strategy includes exposing that.

## How to Tell

Look for the signatures of a captured or investor-friendly process. **A contract granted by a discredited past regime**, as the Gunjur factory's was under the old dictatorship; **a regulator's case against a polluter quietly settled or withdrawn**, as happened in Gunjur; **clear, tested harm ignored** because the investment is wanted; **tax privileges or favours for foreign investors** in exchange for jobs; and **the community's evidence and voice bypassed**. Trace the contract and the enforcement: how it was granted, who profits, and whether the harm and the community were weighed. In The Gambia, a polluter shielded from its own regulator is worth examining for the interests behind it.

## Who Can Be Captured — and Who Is Harder

Be clear-eyed about where capture reaches. **Parts of the government**, hungry for investment, can favour a polluter over a community. But some forces are far harder to capture, and they are your targets: the **courts**, slow but independent; the **free-er press** of the new democracy; the **community's own science**, which money cannot fake; the **community reserve** and the standing it gives; the **international organisations and press**, beyond a government's reach; and the **community** itself, now free to speak. Distinctively, the independent science of a community and the attention of the wider world are checks that investor money finds hard to overcome. Your task is to document the harm and the favour, and bring them to the courts, the press, the international allies, and the public.

## What to Do

Do not assume an investor-shielded polluter cannot be challenged. Instead, **document it precisely** — the tested harm, the contract, the withdrawn enforcement, the interest served — and take it to the forces that can act: the **courts**; the **free press**; the **international organisations**; and the **public**. Pair the exposure with the community science and the new freedom, so the harm and the favour arrive together. In The Gambia, exposing a polluter shielded by a government that wants its money, and pressing the tested harm upon the courts, the press, and the world, is often the way to force accountability — as the exposure of the Gunjur factory's pollution and the government's intervention for it drew national and international attention and real pressure. The pairing of the tested harm and the exposed favour is what makes this hard for a government to weather: the harm alone it might dismiss as a local complaint, and the favour alone it might defend as ordinary investment policy, but the two together — a proven poisoning of a community’s reserve, and a government shielding the polluter from its own regulator — tell a story of a state failing its own people for an investor’s money, which is exactly the story national and international audiences find hardest to ignore and governments most wish to avoid. Bringing the harm and the favour forward together is therefore often the way to generate the outside pressure that can move a government its own citizens cannot. Do this with care, for challenging the powerful in a young democracy has carried arrest.

The five steps win what can be won when they run together. Here is how they fit across the life of a fight, which turns on the community science, the new freedom, the litigation and regulator, and the livelihood stakes.

Keep the record and the science, unite the community, use your freedom lawfully, press every lever, protect your people, and treat every test taken, every order won, every closure forced, and every precedent set as a real gain, even when the whole cannot be won at once.

The tools are here, and Gunjur itself proved them — partially, and with hard honesty about the cost: a foreign-owned fishmeal factory beside a community's own wildlife reserve, its waste turning the lagoon red and killing the fish a village lived on; and a community, newly free after a dictatorship, that tested the water and proved the poison, pressed the regulator into ordering the factory to stop, took the company to court, protested in the streets, and drew the nation and the world to its cause — forcing real accountability and, amid the wider protest, the temporary closure of two other factories, even as this one kept operating, the government sided with the investment, the case stalled, and protesters faced arrest. What decides the outcome, in the end, is the hardness of the science, the unity of the community, the courage of the newly free, the reach of the world's attention, and the fishery a people will not surrender — and those a determined community can summon. Begin today, begin with the science and the community and the record of the harm, and build from there. Communities before you have made a foreign polluter answer, even without shutting it down. What Gunjur won can be won again. So, in time and with care, can yours.

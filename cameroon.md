# HOW COMMUNITIES CAN STOP DESTRUCTIVE DEVELOPMENT PROJECTS IN CAMEROON

*Complete Community Opposition How-To*

*This guide is built around Cameroon's distinctive lever — documenting what a land deal truly is, refusing the consent it claims, exposing its illegalities, and carrying that exposure from the village to the world. It names the bodies and terms you'll meet (the lease and land classification, the EIA and public consultation, the certification standards a big investor needs, the courts) in plain words. Civic space here is constrained and this work can be dangerous, so safety runs throughout.*

# INTRODUCTION & FRAMEWORK

In Cameroon's Southwest rainforest, a New York agribusiness — **Herakles Farms**, through its local arm **SGSOC** — planned an oil-palm plantation of some **seventy thousand hectares**: the forest home and farmland of tens of thousands of people across dozens of villages, sitting among a cluster of national parks and reserves, nearly all of it dense natural forest full of endangered chimpanzees and drills. The communities did not want their forest cleared and their land taken — and they turned it back. With the Cameroonian organisation **SEFE** (Struggle to Economize Future Environment) and international allies beside them, they **attacked the deal's story rather than only its bulldozers.** A land deal like this rests on a claim — that the land is idle, that the company acts lawfully, that the communities consent and benefit. The campaign showed every part of that claim to be false: they **mapped** the concession and proved it was living forest, not idle land; they **documented** the illegal logging and the land taken without real consent; and they **refused** to let the company mark out their land. That exposure stripped the investor of the legitimacy it needed to keep its financiers, its certifiers, and its government backers. The company's standing collapsed, the government **suspended** the project, the concession was **cut to a fraction** of its size, and in time the company **abandoned** its operations. The forest, and the villages, were largely spared.

SEFE and the forest communities turned back Herakles Farms by attacking the deal's story rather than only its bulldozers — and the tools they used are available, if constrained, to communities across Cameroon. A large project needs a **land lease**, over land whose classification and status can be **contested**. It needs an **environmental impact assessment** with public consultation. Big agribusiness investors depend on **certification standards** — the RSPO for palm oil — and on **financiers** whose reputations a campaign can reach. And the **courts** can be used, carefully. The decisive move was to aim all of these at the deal's own claim — that the land was idle, the company lawful, the communities consenting — and prove every part of it false: mapping the concession as living forest, documenting the illegal logging and the consent that was never given, and refusing to let the company mark out the land, until the investor lost the legitimacy it needed to keep its financiers and backers. But these tools have real limits, and this guide states them plainly — and this was a hard, dangerous, only partial victory. The win was **partial and gradual** — a concession cut down and abandoned over years, with real forest lost and timber illegally logged before it ended — and it leaned partly on the **company's own failures** and collapsing finances. And, most gravely, the fight was **dangerous**: in Cameroon's constrained civic space, the leader of the organisation at its heart was arrested, held, and suffered violence for his activism — not a rare misfortune but a foreseeable cost of challenging the powerful here. A community must plan for safety as deliberately as it plans its case, and decide, with open eyes, what risks it is prepared to bear.

## The Whole Strategy in One Idea

A big project looks unstoppable, but it rests on a story — idle land, lawful conduct, community consent — and that story is a chain of claims, each of which can be shown false. A large Cameroonian land project needs its **lease** and land classification; its **EIA**; the **consent** it claims; and the legitimacy it needs to keep its **certifiers, financiers, and government backers**. Every one of those is a link you can grab — and the deal's false story is the weakest. **Find the one closest to your hand and pull** — safely.

## Be Honest About Which Fight You're In

Before you spend a year on this, work out your real situation:

- **If the deal's story is false — living forest called idle, illegal logging, consent never given — documenting and exposing that is your sharpest lever.**
- **If it needs certification or foreign finance, those are pressure points** — a certifier or a financier will not stay with an exposed illegality.
- **If your community refuses its consent and refuses to let the land be marked out, that refusal is real power.**
- **Always: this is dangerous work in a constrained space.** Plan for safety first — spread leadership, keep international attention on those most at risk, act in numbers and non-violently.

Read the whole guide once before you move. Two habits run through it: **attack the deal's story with documentation and refusal**, and **carry it from the village to the world** — the community, the evidence, the coalition, the exposure, all pressed together. That is how the Herakles plantation was turned back.

# HOW THE SYSTEM WORKS

To break the chain you have to see it — who decides, how a project moves, and who's watching.

## Who Actually Decides

A large land project needs a **lease** granted by the state over land whose **classification** (forest, farmland, protected) shapes what is lawful; an **EIA** approved by the environment ministry after public consultation; and, in practice, the acceptance of its **certifiers** (industry standards bodies), its **financiers**, and its **government backers**. Local **chiefs and communities** hold customary authority over the land and its use. Know which of these you can reach — and remember that the legitimacy the investor needs from all of them is itself the target.

## How a Project Moves — and Where You Jump In

**The lease and land classification.** The state grants the lease; the land's classification (e.g. forest that should not be cleared) constrains it. *Your opening:* show the land is living forest or community farmland, not idle land — attacking the deal's founding claim.

**The EIA and consultation.** The project needs an EIA with public consultation. *Your opening:* take part, document the harm, and show the consultation was never genuine.

**Consent and the marking of land.** The company claims community consent and moves to mark out the land. *Your opening — powerful:* refuse consent, collectively, and refuse to let the land be marked.

**Operation.** *Your opening:* document illegal logging and clearing, and expose it to certifiers, financiers, and the world.

## Follow the Money and the Permissions

Dig up three things. **Who's behind it and who owns them** — including any foreign parent, whose reputation and finances are pressure points (Herakles's collapsed). **What legitimacy it depends on** — a certification, a financier, a government backer that will not stay with an exposed illegality. **What its story claims** — idle land, lawful conduct, consent — because each claim is a link you can break.

## Who's Supposed to Be Watching

Each is an ally or an arena — some to persuade, some to expose to. The **environment ministry** runs the EIA. The **certification standards bodies** (like the RSPO) can withdraw a company's legitimacy. The **financiers** can pull out. The **courts** can be used carefully. And Cameroon's **environmental and community organisations, its chiefs, and international allies and press** are the forces that reach furthest — the coalition that turned back Herakles.

# STEP 1: TARGET IDENTIFICATION

Being against "the project" is weak. Being specific — this false claim, this illegal logging, this consent never given — is strong. Answer five questions in writing, safely.

**1. What is it, and who's behind it?** Name the project, the company, and its foreign parent and financiers — and judge the risks.

**2. What is the land really, and what does it touch?** Establish the truth of the land — **living forest, community farmland, near protected areas** — against the deal's claim that it's idle. **The true nature of the land is your founding lever**, as the Herakles mapping was.

**3. What does its story claim, and where is it false?** Idle land? Lawful conduct? Community consent and benefit? Identify each claim and where it's false.

**4. What legitimacy does it depend on?** A certification, a financier, a government backer, the community's consent — each a pressure point.

**5. Who else is affected, and who already opposes it — and who can you ally with safely?** The villages, the chiefs, national organisations, international allies. Herakles united dozens of villages with organisations and the world.

**What this looks like in real life.** A community learns a plantation is planned on its forest, claimed as idle land, with logging already starting and "consent" being manufactured. The five questions yield: a company and its foreign parent; living forest, not idle land (the founding lever); a false story of idle land, lawful conduct, and consent; legitimacy resting on certifiers and financiers; and villages, chiefs, and allies to unite — safely. That's the Herakles pattern — break the story, refuse consent, expose it to the world.

# STEP 2: DOCUMENTATION

The difference between a community that can fight and one that can't is proof — and Herakles was won by turning the company's own story into the evidence against it. Build it in three layers, safely.

**Layer one: prove the land is not what they claim.** Map and photograph the concession to show it is **living forest or community farmland**, not idle land — with the wildlife, the villages, the farms, the protected areas nearby. This breaks the deal's founding claim, as the Herakles mapping did.

**Layer two: document the illegalities and the false consent.** Record the **illegal logging and clearing**, the land taken **without real consent**, the "consultations" that never genuinely informed the community, and any breaches of the lease or the law — dated, located, and kept securely.

**Layer three: the paper trail and the map of harm.** Get the lease, the land classification, the EIA, and the consultation record, and lay the project over the forest, the villages, and the protected areas. Walking into a certifier, a financier, the press, or a court with the concession mapped, the illegalities documented, and the false story exposed is what makes them act.

# WHAT TO GATHER, AND WHERE TO FIND IT

- **The lease and land classification, the EIA, the consultation record** — from the state, the environment ministry, and the company's own materials.
- **Proof the land is living forest/farmland** — maps, photographs, wildlife and village evidence.
- **Documentation of illegalities and false consent** — of illegal logging, clearing, and manufactured "consent."
- **Ownership, finance, and certification** — the foreign parent, the financiers, and the certification standards the company courts.
- **Dated, secure evidence and testimony** — marked with date and place, gathered so no one is put at risk.

# STEP 3: BUILDING LOCAL OPPOSITION

Documents give you a case; a united community and its allies give you power — and Herakles united villages, organisations, and the world. Build in four moves — and safely.

**Start with a tight, trusted core.** The villages and chiefs most affected, the people who know the forest, a few tireless documenters, and respected local figures.

**Unite the villages and refuse consent together.** The company needs consent it can claim — so decide collectively to **refuse it**, and refuse to let the land be marked out. A united refusal breaks the consent claim at the heart of the deal.

**Build the coalition from the village to the world.** Reach Cameroonian organisations like **SEFE**, and international allies and press who can expose a foreign investor and reach its certifiers and financiers. This alliance, village to world, is Cameroon's distinctive strength.

**Plan safety as deliberately as the case.** This is the gravest point. In a constrained civic space, exposing a powerful company is the act most likely to draw retaliation, and the leaders — organisers, chiefs, documenters — are most exposed. **Spread leadership** so no one person is the single target, keep **international attention on those most at risk**, act always **in numbers and non-violently**, keep a secure archive, and decide together what risks you will bear.

# STEP 4: LEGAL, CERTIFICATION & CONSENT LEVERS

Cameroon's sharpest levers are not only the courts but the deal's own dependencies — its consent, its certification, its finance.

## Refuse Consent — and Break the Claim

The company's story depends on **community consent**. A collective, documented **refusal of consent**, and a refusal to let the land be marked out, breaks that claim and denies the investor the social licence it needs. This is one of your most powerful and direct levers — exercised in numbers, non-violently, and safely.

## Certification, Financiers, and Exposure

Big agribusiness investors depend on **certification** (like the RSPO for palm oil) and on **financiers** with reputations to protect. Take your documented illegalities — the living forest called idle, the illegal logging, the false consent — to the certifiers and financiers: **they will not stay with an exposed illegality**, and their withdrawal, as at Herakles, can collapse the project's legitimacy and its finances.

## The EIA and the Courts

Take part in the **EIA** and consultation, and document their flaws. And, carefully, the **courts** can be used to challenge an unlawful lease, a false land classification, or illegal clearing — though in a constrained space, weigh the risks and lean on documentation and exposure as much as litigation.

## The Honest Limits — and Safety

Straight talk: these levers are real and turned back Herakles, but the win was partial and gradual, leaned on the company's own collapse, and was won at real personal risk. A determined, state-backed project is hard to stop outright, and the danger to leaders is grave. So use the strong tools — the false-story documentation, the consent refusal, the certifiers and financiers, the EIA and courts — carry them to the world, protect your people above all, and count a suspended or cut-down concession, an abandoned project, delay, and exposure as wins.

# TURNING YOUR EVIDENCE INTO ARGUMENTS

A stack of documents wins nothing until you aim it.

**"Idle land" becomes living forest.** Not "we oppose the plantation" but *"this is dense natural forest, home to endangered chimpanzees and our farms — not the idle land the company claims."*

**Logging becomes an exposed illegality.** *"The company is logging and clearing illegally — here is the documentation."*

**"Consent" becomes a refusal.** *"We never consented; our communities refuse this deal and refuse to let our land be marked."*

**The false story becomes a case to the certifiers and the world.** *"An investor calling living forest idle, logging illegally, and faking our consent — a scandal its certifiers and financiers cannot ignore."*

Match it to the venue: the true land and the illegalities to the certifiers, financiers, and press; the refusal to the company and the state; the flaws to the EIA; the unlawful lease to the courts, carefully.

# STEP 5: MEDIA STRATEGY

In Cameroon, international exposure is the decisive lever, because it reaches a foreign investor's certifiers, financiers, and reputation — and, for leaders at risk, it is also protection. Community organisations and international press make the media front central, handled with the greatest care.

**Lead with the forest, the people, and the false story.** Not "the EIA was deficient" but "living forest, home to endangered chimpanzees and tens of thousands of people, called idle land and cleared illegally for a plantation we never agreed to." Use your strongest material: the concession map, dated photographs, documented illegalities, and community voices.

**Work several audiences at once — reach the certifiers and financiers.** Local and national attention builds pressure; international media, NGO networks, and the certification bodies reach the investor where it is most exposed. Make it easy: captioned photos, accurate summaries, a map, careful testimony.

**Two hard rules.** Accuracy above all — one exaggeration can discredit everything and invite a lawsuit or worse. And protect people — international attention shields those at risk, so keep it on them; weigh every public act; and let the community lead its own story.

# EMAILS & LETTERS

Make them your own; keep them factual and calm; keep secure copies; and protect anyone at risk.

**8B — Refusing consent (to the company / state).**
*Subject: We do not consent — [project].*
"We, the communities of [place], do not consent to [project] on our land, which is living forest and our farmland, not idle land. We refuse to let our land be marked out. Any claim of our consent is false. [Contact.]"

**8C — To a certification body.**
*Subject: Documented illegalities — [project] / [company].*
"We report that [company]'s [project] at [location] clears living forest, logs illegally, and claims a consent it never obtained: [specifics]. We ask you to withhold or withdraw certification. [Attach evidence.]"

**8D — To a financier.**
*Subject: Legal, environmental, and reputational risk — [project].*
"We understand your institution may finance [project] at [location], which clears protected forest, logs illegally, and lacks our consent, and faces exposure and challenge. As with Herakles, this carries real risk. [Contact and evidence summary.]"

**8E — Objection in the EIA / consultation.**
*Subject: Objection — EIA for [project].*
"We, the communities of [place], object to [project]: [the land is living forest, not idle; the EIA understates the harm; the consultation never genuinely reached us]. We ask that it be refused. [Attach evidence.]"

**8F — To a lawyer / organisation (carefully).**
*Subject: Unlawful land deal — [project].*
"Our communities face [project] on our forest, claimed as idle land, with illegal logging and false consent. We've gathered [the map, the documentation]. We ask for your help and alliance — and advice on safety. [Contact and evidence summary.]"

**8G — Letter to the (international) press.**
*Subject: Communities of [place] act to protect their forest from [project].*
"For generations the people of [place] have lived from [this forest]. Now [project], by [company], calls it idle land and clears it — without our consent. We can provide the concession map, documented illegalities, photographs, and — carefully — interviews. [Contact.]"

**8H — Uniting the community (safely).**
*Subject: We can protect our forest — here's how.*
"[Project] threatens our forest. As the Herakles fight showed, if we prove our land is living forest, refuse our consent, and carry the illegalities to the world, we can turn back even a giant plantation. Let's document, refuse together, and stand as one — carefully, protecting our leaders. [Contact.]"

# IF YOU HAVE LITTLE TIME OR FEW RESOURCES: THE RAPID-FIRE VERSION

If you must move fast and lean, do these six things, in order, and safely:

1. **Prove the land is living forest or your farmland, not idle land (map it).** That breaks the deal's founding claim.
2. **Refuse consent together, and refuse to let your land be marked (8B).**
3. **Document the illegal logging and false consent — securely.**
4. **Take the documentation to the certifiers and financiers (8C/8D), and object in the EIA (8E).**
5. **Carry it to the international press and allies (8G) — which also protects your leaders.**
6. **Plan safety deliberately — spread leadership, act in numbers, keep attention on those at risk (8H).**

Even lean, those six break the false story, refuse the consent, and expose the deal — the tools that turned back Herakles — while protecting people.

# WHEN THE SYSTEM IS TILTED

Cameroon's tools are real, but the field is tilted and the space constrained, so plan for it — and stay safe.

**When the state backs the project.** Don't rely on the domestic process alone: attack the deal's story, refuse consent, and reach the certifiers, financiers, and the world, where the state's backing doesn't protect the investor.

**When the win is only partial.** Herakles was cut down and abandoned over years, with forest lost first. Keep documenting and pressing; a partial win can grow, and the company's own failures may finish it.

**When leaders are targeted — the gravest tilt.** This is central, not incidental. Spread leadership, keep international attention on those at risk, act in numbers and non-violently, and decide together what risks you will bear. Never let the project matter more than a life.

**When consultation is faked.** Document the fake; it breaks the consent claim and is a scandal for the certifiers.

# WHEN IT'S ACTUAL CORRUPTION — AND WHO'S CAPTURED

Cameroon has serious, documented problems with the capture of land and forest deals, so this matters — handled with great care given the danger.

**How to spot it.** Leases against the land's true classification; living forest called idle; illegal logging tolerated; "consent" manufactured; benefits to the connected while communities lose their forest.

**Who's supposed to guard against it.** Anti-corruption and audit bodies examine public money and conduct. The **courts** can void an unlawful lease. And — most effectively here — **international certifiers, financiers, and press** can strip a captured deal of its legitimacy, as at Herakles.

**How to fight it safely.** Document precisely and factually — the false classification, the illegal logging, the faked consent — and keep strictly to what you can prove. Route serious evidence to the certifiers, financiers, international bodies, and trusted journalists rather than making accusations you can't back — the retaliation risk is grave. Protect those exposed, and keep international attention on them.

# INTEGRATION & TIMELINE

Don't run these steps one after another — run them together, safely. The documents break the false story; the refusal of consent denies the licence; the coalition from village to world is the muscle; the certifiers, financiers, EIA, and courts press the case; the international media raises the cost and protects your people. Together they multiply. One at a time, they stall.

**Early on (weeks 1–8):** map the land to prove it's living forest; refuse consent together; document the illegalities securely; object in the EIA; build the coalition, village to world; plan safety deliberately.

**In the thick of it (months–years):** take the documented illegalities to the certifiers and financiers; open the international media front; use the courts carefully; keep refusing consent and marking.

**The long haul (years):** carry the exposure and any litigation; keep the coalition and the international attention alive; hold the line for a suspended or cut-down concession, an abandoned project, delay, and exposure — and keep your leaders safe throughout.

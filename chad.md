# HOW COMMUNITIES CAN STOP DESTRUCTIVE DEVELOPMENT PROJECTS IN CHAD

# INTRODUCTION & FRAMEWORK

Chad's environmental life turns above all on **Lake Chad**, the great transboundary lake it shares with Nigeria, Niger, and Cameroon, on which tens of millions depend for fishing, farming, and herding. The lake shrank drastically in the droughts of the 1970s and 1980s and became a symbol of a warming world; but the honest, more recent science is that it is not simply vanishing — it fluctuates with the rains, has partly recovered, and its communities have long adapted to its rising and falling. The real crisis of the basin is not a lake quietly disappearing but a tangle of variable water, deep poverty, and a long insurgency that has displaced millions — a tangle no single fix can cut. Into this has come **Transaqua**, a vast proposal to refill the lake by diverting water from the distant Congo basin, warned by many to be a costly, risky scheme that could devastate the Congo basin and flood Chad's own lands. And Chad has **oil**, whose pipeline was once a famous experiment in turning oil money to the good of the people — an experiment that failed. The nation is poor and under military rule, and much of the region is insecure. But the truth about the lake, and the scrutiny of the grand fixes, give real ground.

Those who would defend the basin's people work with real, if constrained, tools, all worked carefully and grounded in truth. The strongest is **honest science**: the accurate hydrology of Lake Chad, which fluctuates rather than simply vanishes, is itself a corrective to the false premise behind the risky grand fix. That grand scheme — **Transaqua**, the proposed diversion of Congo-basin water — can and must be **rigorously scrutinised** through environmental review and the transboundary frameworks, for its transboundary harm, its flood risk to Chad's own lands, and its questionable premise. The **transboundary lake and river frameworks** — the Lake Chad Basin Commission, the international water regimes, and the Congo basin's own stake — govern the shared waters and reach the schemes. The **oil and its broken revenue promises and community harms** can be held to account, through its financiers and the record of a failed experiment. And the **humanitarian stakes of the basin** reach the world and its conscience — a channel and a lever. But these tools have grave limits, and this guide states them plainly. The lake's story is complex and its science contested, so honest science and adaptation, not a single dramatic rescue, are the realistic path. The poverty and the long insurgency are real and beyond easy remedy, and much of the region is insecure and dangerous. The nation is poor and under military rule. And, above all, the vulnerable communities and any local advocates must be protected, and the conflict zones avoided entirely. And though Lake Chad and the grand fixes define the struggle here, the same careful tools reach any other harmful project — environmental review, the functioning institutions and transboundary frameworks, and honest science — the lake remaining the central case. So the work must be careful, honest-science-grounded, and framed around the livelihoods and the frameworks, never around challenging authority — and the honest prize is a risky scheme scrutinised, the oil held to account, and the lake's true livelihoods defended, not a project stopped by confrontation.

# HOW COMMUNITIES CAN STOP DESTRUCTIVE DEVELOPMENT PROJECTS IN MALI

# INTRODUCTION & FRAMEWORK

Mali's life flows from the Niger River and, above all, from the **Inner Niger Delta** at its heart — one of the world's great inland wetlands, which sustains some one and a half million people, yields the great majority of the nation's fish and half its dry-season livestock, and grows its distinctive floating rice — a lifeline now threatened at once by dams proposed upstream, by the diversion of its water for large-scale irrigation, and by a deepening, climate-driven drought. At the same time, Mali is one of Africa's great gold producers, and its gold, dug both in large industrial mines and by countless small-scale miners across the land, is won with mercury and cyanide that poison the rivers, the soil, and the health of those who live by them. This is a poor nation under military rule and gripped by a grinding conflict, with insecurity across much of its land, armed groups, and real dangers to those who speak out. There is no single victory to be won. But the work can be done, carefully, and the levers are real.

In a poor nation under military rule and gripped by conflict, those who would defend Mali's water and land work carefully, through community, technical, and international channels — and the tools are real. The distinctive lever is the **case against the poison of the gold**: the mercury and cyanide fouling the rivers, soil, and people's health are reached through the global **Minamata mercury framework** and the concrete, non-ideological health evidence — the aim being to clean the gold up, not to end the livelihood of millions. The **Inner Niger Delta that feeds millions** can be defended through the river's shared, transboundary governance — the Niger Basin frameworks — since its threats come partly from dams upstream in another country, and through its **Ramsar wetland** protection. The **livelihoods** the water sustains — the fishing, farming, and herding of a million and a half people — are a powerful, unifying, non-ideological stake. And the **international frameworks and partners**, together with the company and financier standards and the donor leverage a poor, aid-dependent state affords, reach the miners, the companies, and the state. But these tools have grave limits, and this guide states them plainly. Gold is a mainstay of the nation and the livelihood of millions, so the poor miners must never be punished — only helped to safer ways. The delta's threats come partly from upstream in another country and from Mali's own need for food and power, beyond its sole control. And — this matters most of all — the nation is poor, under military rule, and beset by insecurity and conflict, with armed groups and real dangers to those who speak out, so the work must be done with the greatest care, through community, technical, and international channels, framed around health, water, and livelihoods, never around challenging authority, and protecting everyone — especially the vulnerable communities and local advocates — absolutely. So the honest prize is the poison reduced through the mercury framework and the health case, the delta defended through the shared river governance and its wetland status, the livelihoods protected — carefully, through community and international channels, not an open stop. Above all, protect the people.

## The Whole Strategy in One Idea

You cannot mount open confrontation in a nation under military rule and gripped by conflict, and you must never endanger the vulnerable.

# HOW COMMUNITIES CAN STOP DESTRUCTIVE DEVELOPMENT PROJECTS IN HAITI

# INTRODUCTION & FRAMEWORK

Haiti is a mountainous Caribbean nation on the island of Hispaniola, the poorest in the entire western hemisphere, whose defining environmental question is the near-total stripping of its steep hillsides of their trees and then of their soil — driven by the charcoal and fuelwood on which most families depend to cook, and the deadly cascade of erosion, flood, and landslide that has followed. This guide is honest from the outset about a crucial point of justice: the charcoal that drives the deforestation is not greed but survival, the fuel of families with no affordable alternative, so the answer can never be to punish the poor with bans, but to offer alternatives, livelihoods, and community-led restoration. And it must be honest that the state has largely collapsed and much of the territory lies under armed-group control, so this work must run very largely from outside and through the community — the vast diaspora, the international partners, and the local efforts that carry on even amid the crisis — always protecting anyone inside. The tools that remain are real.

In a collapsed state, the tools that remain are external, communal, and protective, and they are real. The **remaining forest** — the national parks and the last forested slopes — can be defended through their protected-area designations and the international conservation partners who work them. Where a formal project threatens — a donor-financed development that displaces farmers, a mine, a dam — the **environmental assessment and, above all, the lender's and donor's safeguards and accountability mechanisms** are a real, reachable lever, as displaced Haitian communities have shown by bringing such claims. The **vast Haitian diaspora and the international partners** carry documentation, funding, and advocacy from safety. **Community stewardship** protects what remains where it is safe, and **rigorous documentation**, gathered only where safe, grounds it all. But these tools have grave limits, and this guide states them plainly. The deforestation is all but complete, and the poverty driving the charcoal economy is profound — so the answer is never bans on the poor, but alternatives and livelihoods, and the deeper environmental work of Haiti is the community's own reforestation and restoration, a matter of life and death in every storm, not a discrete project to be stopped. The state has collapsed, much of the territory lies under armed-group control, and the disasters are recurrent and climate-amplified. So the work must run from outside and through the community, only where it is safe, avoiding the armed-group areas, and protecting anyone inside absolutely. So the honest prize is the remaining forest defended, a harmful donor-financed project held to account, and the community's restoration supported with alternatives — resting on the efforts that continue even now, not a single triumphant stop. Above all, protect the people, and never punish the poor.

## The Whole Strategy in One Idea

You cannot rely on a collapsed state, and you must never punish the poor whose charcoal is survival. What can be done is to defend what remains and hold formal harms to account, from outside and through the community: protect the last forests through their designations and the international conservation partners, hold any donor-financed project to its lender's safeguards and accountability mechanisms, work through the diaspora and international partners, and support the community's own restoration with alternatives — all only where it is safe, and protecting everyone.

# HOW THE SYSTEM WORKS

A diffuse harm — the charcoal frontier, the erosion — is met not by stopping a project but by the community's restoration and by defending what forest remains. Enter through the designations, the safeguards, and the international partners, and only where it is safe.

# STEP 1: TARGET IDENTIFICATION

Pick the harm where an external, safe lever — a designation, a safeguard, a partner — can be reached.

A case built on the designations and the lenders' safeguards, gathered safely, is one the external partners can act on.

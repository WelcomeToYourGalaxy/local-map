Secure migration with dignity and the right to remain. Hold the emitters to account. Raise the land carefully. And honour the people's dignity and choice — because for a nation facing erasure through no fault of its own, a defence that fixes its existence in law, preserves its identity, demands justice, and upholds its people's dignity is the one that is both effective and just.

Tuvalu is among the lowest and most exposed nations on earth, nine coral atolls barely two metres above a sea that is rising to claim them, threatened with erasure by emissions its people did not produce and cannot stop; this guide has hidden none of that, nor pretended that the sea can be halted, or that the defences are without cost, or that the future is secure — the science is grim, and there is no victory to be won over the threat itself. But Tuvalu has refused to accept erasure, and in refusing has begun to do something the world has never seen: to fix a nation's statehood and its ocean boundaries in law so they endure beyond the land, to secure for its people a dignified path to migrate while defending their right to remain, to preserve its identity and its government in the digital realm, to hold the great emitters to account in the world's courts, and to raise its very ground against the tide. There is no stopping the sea, and no easy victory; this guide has said so throughout, and insisted on the dignity of a people through an ordeal few nations have faced. But the existence, the rights, the identity, and the dignity of a nation are worth defending even when its territory cannot yet be saved, the levers to defend them are real and even historic, and the patient, dignified, many-fronted work of defending a homeland's very existence is one that Tuvaluans have already begun, and shown the world how to do. So can you carry it forward.

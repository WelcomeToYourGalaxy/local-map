And act with care, protecting your people above all — because in a fragile, dangerous land whose ancient wetlands the world has claimed, an effort that holds the world to that heritage while keeping its people safe is the one that is both effective and just.

Iraq is the land between the rivers, where the world's first cities rose from the marshes of the south, and where the Marsh Arabs have fished and herded and built their reed houses since the days of Sumer — and now those marshes are dying, starved by dams beyond Iraq's borders, parched by a deepening drought, salted and poisoned and pressed by oil, while the flares of the oil fields burn over the cities of the south; this guide has hidden none of it, nor pretended that a land living on oil and robbed of its water by its neighbours holds all the cards, and there is no landmark community victory here to promise you. But the marshes are the heritage of all humanity, recognised by the world and already defended by it — heritage pressure has halted the oil rigs in parts of the wetlands, the world's restorers have brought water and life back before, and the oil companies, the shared-river norms, and the rights of an ancient people are all levers a community can reach. There is no easy victory, and no landmark precedent to inherit; this guide has said so throughout, and insisted on the safety of your people above all. But your marshes, your rivers, your ancient culture, and your children's air are worth defending, the world's claim on this heritage is a real and powerful lever, and the patient, careful work of documenting the loss, holding the world to the heritage it has recognised, pressing the oil companies, and restoring what can be saved is one your community can genuinely do — and keep the marshes of Sumer alive for the generations to come. So can you.

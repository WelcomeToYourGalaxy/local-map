# HOW COMMUNITIES CAN STOP DESTRUCTIVE DEVELOPMENT PROJECTS IN MACAU

# INTRODUCTION & FRAMEWORK

Macau is a tiny, wealthy, hyper-dense special administrative region on the western edge of the Pearl River estuary — the world's largest gambling hub, an economy built on casinos and a landmass built, in great part, on sea reclaimed for them. It is barely thirty-odd square kilometres, much of it conquered from the water, one of the most densely populated places on earth, its skyline crowded with mega-resorts on the reclaimed Cotai strip, its old Portuguese-era heart a World Heritage site nearly swallowed by the growth around it, its waters and air part of one of the most industrialised deltas in the world, its low coast exposed to typhoon and rising sea. This is a wealthy and capable but politically constrained territory, its civic space limited and narrowing. The technical and regional channels realistically open here are real.

A community in Macau facing a harmful project works with real, if constrained, tools, in a wealthy but politically limited territory — technical, inside the system, and regional rather than a mass movement. Major reclamation and projects must reckon with **environmental assessment**, and the **subsidence monitoring and technical evidence** on the reclaimed land are a real, scientific handle on the risks of building on conquered sea. The old Portuguese-era heart is shielded by **World Heritage protection**, a designation that constrains what the growth may swallow. The government's **own diversification and sustainability goals** are commitments a wealthy state can be held to, on its own terms. The **Greater Bay Area and regional cooperation frameworks** are the real channel for the delta's pollution, which is largely regional and beyond Macau alone. And the **technical and academic bodies and the international heritage and environmental standards** carry the case. But these tools have real limits, and this guide states them plainly. The civic and political space here is limited and narrowing, so the work must be technical, inside the system, and regional rather than a mass movement, kept strictly to the environmental and neutral on the politics, and protective of those involved. The casino monoculture is the economy, and the reclamation is driven by land-scarcity and gaming alike. The delta's pollution is largely regional, and the water and energy come mostly from the mainland, so Macau cannot act alone. So aim for what these tools can genuinely deliver — a reclamation scrutinised through the assessment and the subsidence evidence, the heritage defended through its World Heritage protection, the state held to its own sustainability goals, the delta's pollution pressed through the regional frameworks — kept strictly technical and inside the system, in a constrained but capable territory.

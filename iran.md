# INTRODUCTION & FRAMEWORK

Concern for the environment in Iran turns above all on water, and on a hard truth the state itself has come to acknowledge: the country is draining its rivers, lakes, and aquifers faster than nature can replace them, and the cause is less the drought and the warming climate, real as those are, than decades of its own mismanagement — the unchecked building of dams, the over-pumping of groundwater through a million wells, the diversion of water between basins, and the relentless expansion of thirsty agriculture. A great lake once the largest in the Middle East has been reduced to a bed of salt; a storied river runs dry through a historic city; hundreds of plains are sinking as the aquifers beneath them collapse; and the exposed lakebeds send salt and dust across the country, choking its cities' air. Alongside the water, the ancient Hyrcanian forests of the Caspian coast, a treasure recognised by the world, are scorched by drought and fire. The political space is closed and repressive, and environmental work in Iran has carried genuine danger. But the crisis is one the state itself has recognised, and that opens a narrow, real space.

And **Iran's strong national science and the international scientific frameworks** carry the technical case. But these tools have grave limits, and this guide states them plainly.

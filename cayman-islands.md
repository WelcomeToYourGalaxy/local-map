# INTRODUCTION & FRAMEWORK

When the government pressed ahead in 2019 with a roughly **two-hundred-million-dollar cruise berthing and cargo port** in George Town Harbour — a project that would dredge more than **twenty acres of marine habitat** and destroy signature coral reefs and dive sites like **Eden Rock, Devil's Grotto, and Soto's South** — Caymanians did not simply accept it. A grassroots group, **Cruise Port Referendum (CPR) Cayman**, launched the territory's **first people-initiated referendum**, gathering thousands of signatures to force a public vote under the Constitution. They marshalled the marine science on the reefs and the sand that builds Cayman's beaches, questioned the project's value for money, and — when the government campaigned against the very idea of a people's vote — took it to court and **won a ruling that the campaign must be fair and objective**, a court finding the government's messaging little short of propaganda. The project was **shelved in 2020**, and when the question finally reached a national ballot in **April 2025, the public rejected cruise berthing decisively, with more than sixty-three percent voting against**.

CPR Cayman drew on tools available to communities across the Cayman Islands. The **people-initiated referendum** — a petition of a set fraction of the electorate forces a public vote under the Constitution — is the distinctive one. The **courts** enforce the law and fair process, as they did when they ruled the government's referendum messaging must be fair and objective; protest, petition, and litigation are lawful. The **National Conservation Law** and the marine environment carry protection. **Hard marine science** on the reefs, the dive sites, and the sand that builds the beaches is a powerful argument. And a **value-for-money** case bites in a small territory. But these tools have real limits, and this guide states them plainly. The win was real but not simple: the original project's shelving in 2020 owed much to the **COVID-19 pandemic**, which halted cruising and strained government finances, as well as to the campaign — the two came together, and honesty requires naming both. The decisive 2025 referendum was **government-initiated and non-binding**, unlike the binding people-initiated referendum the campaign had fought for — a public verdict, not a legal bar. And cruise berthing has been debated in Cayman for more than a decade and **could return** in another form. So aim for what these levers can deliver around a project the whole territory has a stake in, and stay vigilant, because a shelved project can come back.

## The Whole Strategy in One Idea

A big project looks unstoppable, but it's a chain of approvals and public consent, and a chain breaks at its weakest link. A Cayman fight turns on the **people-initiated referendum** that can force a public vote; on the **courts** that enforce fair process and the conservation law; on the **marine science** that proves the reef harm; on the **value-for-money** case; and on the **mobilised public**. Every one of those is a link you can grab — and the referendum is the one that reaches highest. **Find the one closest to your hand and pull.**

**3. What does it cost and return?** The price, the value for money, the benefit — because a bad deal is a damning, vote-winning argument.

**4. What stage is it at, and can you force a vote?** Is an approval or assessment in process? Can you gather the petition for a **people-initiated referendum**? Is the government campaigning unfairly (the courts)? These are the openings.

**5. Who's your coalition?** A grassroots group (like CPR Cayman), the dive industry, marine scientists, and the public. Cayman joined all of these.

# TURNING YOUR EVIDENCE INTO ARGUMENTS

A stack of documents wins nothing until you aim it.

**Reef destruction becomes a vote.** Not "we oppose the port" but *"this dredges twenty acres and destroys Eden Rock and Devil's Grotto — let the people vote on it."*

**A bad deal becomes a public argument.** *"Two hundred million dollars of debt for a project that destroys our reefs and returns little — is that value for money?"*

**Government propaganda becomes a court case.** *"The government must run a fair, objective campaign — not propaganda,"* as Cayman's court ruled.

**A shelving becomes a warning.** *"It was shelved, not killed — and could return; we stay ready."*

Match it to the venue: the reef science and the economics to the referendum and the public; the fair-process and conservation case to the courts; the shelving to continued vigilance.

# STEP 5: MEDIA STRATEGY

In the Cayman Islands, public opinion is decisive, because the people-initiated referendum puts the vote in the public's hands — and the reefs and dive economy are a national cause. A free press makes the media front central.

**Lead with the reefs and the bad deal, not procedure.** Not "the assessment was deficient" but "our reefs and dive sites destroyed, and two hundred million dollars of debt, for a cruise port we don't need." Use your strongest material: the marine science, the value-for-money case, the dive sites by name, and the vote to come.

**Work several audiences at once.** Caymanian press and public win the referendum; international attention (the world-famous dive sites, the cruise industry's reputation) reaches beyond the islands. Make it easy: clear science, the cost figures, images of the reefs and dive sites.

**Two hard rules.** Accuracy above all — one exaggeration can discredit the science. And keep it a clear public choice — the referendum is won on a compelling, honest case the public can vote on.

# WHEN IT'S ACTUAL CORRUPTION — AND WHO'S CAPTURED

The Cayman Islands have real concerns about the closeness of large developers and the cruise industry to decisions, so this matters.

**How to spot it.** Assessments that understate the reef harm; contracts favouring the connected; a bad-value deal pushed regardless; benefits to the connected while the reefs and public bear the cost.

**Who's supposed to guard against it.** Cayman's audit and oversight bodies examine public money and conduct. The **courts** can enforce fair process and quash a tainted approval. The **National Conservation** authorities guard the reefs. And Cayman's **grassroots groups, the dive industry, and a free press** have exposed bad deals.

**How to fight it safely.** Document precisely and factually — how the assessment understated the harm, how the deal favours the connected, what it costs — and keep clear the line between what you can prove and what you suspect. Route serious evidence to the audit bodies, the courts, and trusted journalists rather than making accusations you can't back. Pair the exposure with the referendum and the science.

Libya is a vast desert nation on the Mediterranean, drinking from an ancient aquifer that will not refill through the largest artificial river ever built,Libya is a vast desert nation on the southern Mediterranean shore, drinking from an ancient, prehistoric aquifer that will not refill through the largest artificial river ever built, divided between rival governments, its oil the contested prize of the fracture, its neglected dams having drowned thousands at Derna in a night. This guide has hidden none of that — the division, the finite fossil water, the ageing and deadly infrastructure, the entangled oil — and there is no swift or landmark victory to promise you. But Libya's is a crisis of management as much as scarcity, and its institutions still function: the water and River authorities can maintain and conserve, the engineers can secure the dams, the international and UN bodies can support and coordinate, and the aquifer, though finite, can be stewarded rather than squandered. Those are the levers, and they are real: the maintenance and conservation that safeguard a finite water lifeline; the dam-safety that Derna's dead demand; the transparency and international engagement that would direct the nation's oil wealth toward its water and its people's safety; and the transboundary cooperation over an aquifer four nations share. There is no unified state to petition, no refilling of the ancient water, and no work that is not shadowed by the division and the danger; this guide has said so throughout, and insisted above all on the safety of everyone. But the water and the dams and the land of Libya are worth every careful, safe, management-centred effort, the levers that run through the functioning institutions and the international are real, and the patient, protective work of safeguarding the water, securing the dams, confronting the oil, and stewarding the scarce and finite water is one that the communities, the functioning institutions, and the international bodies can genuinely do — and, after Derna, save lives in the doing. So can you.

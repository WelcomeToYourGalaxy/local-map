# HOW COMMUNITIES CAN STOP DESTRUCTIVE DEVELOPMENT PROJECTS IN THE SOLOMON ISLANDS

*Complete Community Opposition How-To*

The central problem here is not missing laws but unenforced ones, so much of the fight is forcing the existing law to work.*

# INTRODUCTION & FRAMEWORK

On **Wagina Island** — a remote place of some two thousand people in Choiseul Province, who live by fishing, seaweed farming, and their gardens — a company owned out of Hong Kong, **Solomon Bauxite Limited**, held government approval to dig an open-pit bauxite mine that would have swallowed **sixty percent of the island**, cleared two thousand hectares of forest, and run a hundred and fifty truckloads a day for twenty years, destroying the land, the rivers, and the sea the community lived on. The people of Wagina refused. They fought from 2013, and with the help of legal aid from the **Public Solicitor's office** and an environmental legal team, they brought their case to the country's **Environmental Advisory Committee**. In 2019, for the first time in the nation's history, that committee **revoked the company's development consent** — because the company had never obtained the community's **genuine consent**, and had hidden its environmental impact statement from the people it would affect. When the company appealed, the appeal was dismissed. A small island had beaten a foreign mining company and its own government's approval.

The people of Wagina, with legal aid from the Public Solicitor's office, beat a foreign bauxite miner with a toolkit whose decisive lever is customary land and the right to genuine consent. The great majority of Solomon Islands land is held under **custom** by its people, and a project needs their **free, prior, and informed consent (FPIC)** — so a project that never truly won the community's agreement, or that hid its impacts, is legally and morally vulnerable, as Wagina proved when the **Environmental Advisory Committee** revoked the consent. A project needs an **environmental impact statement** and consent, reviewable by that committee. The **Public Solicitor** and environmental legal teams provide **legal aid**. And the **courts** hear appeals; protest, petition, and litigation are lawful. FPIC is not a foreign import but the natural legal expression of a society where land is held by its people under custom — a company cannot lawfully proceed on a government licence alone; it must win the genuine agreement of the people whose land it is. But these tools have real limits, and this guide states them plainly. The Solomons' central problem is not the absence of laws but their **non-enforcement**, and above all the **corruption of the logging and mining industries** — licences granted on a single staged meeting, consent faked, chiefs and landowners divided and paid. So a community's task is frequently not to invent a new protection but to **force the existing one to work**. Wagina took **seven years** and turned on a clear legal defect — one win against many losses — and the **state is weak and remote**, so a community far from Honiara can struggle to reach the law without help.

A big project looks unstoppable, but it's a chain of approvals and claims, and a chain breaks at its weakest link. A Solomon Islands project needs **your customary land** — so it needs your genuine **FPIC**; it needs its **environmental impact statement and consent**, reviewable by the **Environmental Advisory Committee**; and it can be challenged with **legal aid** and in the **courts**. Every one of those is a link you can grab — and a faked or absent consent is the fatal defect. **Find the one closest to your hand and pull.**

# STEP 1: TARGET IDENTIFICATION

Being against "the project" is weak. Being specific — this faked consent, this hidden impact statement, this customary land — is strong. Answer five questions in writing.

**1. What is it, and who's behind it?** Name the project, the foreign company, and its backers.

**2. Whose land is it on, and what does it touch?** Establish that it's your **customary land** — held under custom by your people — and map it over your gardens, forest, rivers, and sea. **Your customary land and your right to genuine consent are your sharpest levers**, and the land and sea are your very life.

**3. Was your genuine consent ever obtained — or was it faked or absent?** This is the decisive question. A staged meeting, a hidden impact statement, divided chiefs — these are the fatal defects, as at Wagina.

**4. What stage is it at, and what's the next deadline?** Is the impact statement out (or hidden)? Has development consent been granted (opening the Environmental Advisory Committee and the courts)? These openings are time-bound.

**5. Is your community united, and who can help you reach the law?** The customary landowners, the chiefs, the church — held united against division; and the **Public Solicitor** and environmental legal teams. Wagina united its people and drew legal aid.

**What this looks like in real life.** A logging or mining company arrives with a government licence but never truly won the community's consent, and hides its impacts. The five questions yield: a foreign company and its backers; your customary land and livelihoods at stake (your levers); a consent that was faked or absent (the fatal defect); an impact statement or consent to challenge; and a community to unite and legal aid to reach. That's the Wagina pattern — insist on genuine consent, expose the defect, and force the law to work with legal aid.

# STEP 2: DOCUMENTATION

The difference between a community that can fight and one that can't is proof — and at Wagina the decisive proof was that consent was never genuine and the impact statement was hidden. Build it in three layers.

**Layer one: the consent and the paper trail.** Document how **consent was faked or absent** — the staged meeting, the divided or paid chiefs, the people never truly asked — and get the licence, the **environmental impact statement** (or prove it was hidden from you), and the development consent. This is the heart of a Solomon Islands case.

**Layer two: the ground truth.** Dated, located photographs and video of your land, forest, rivers, and sea, and the testimony of the landowners and community. Show what the island's life is, and what the project would destroy.

**Layer three: the map of customary land and harm.** Lay the project over your customary land and your land-and-sea livelihoods, so your ownership and the harm are undeniable. Walking into the Environmental Advisory Committee or the courts — with legal aid, the faked consent exposed, and that map — is what makes them act.

# WHEN IT'S ACTUAL CORRUPTION — AND WHO'S CAPTURED

The Solomon Islands has serious, documented corruption in the logging and mining industries, so this matters.

**How to spot it.** Licences on a single staged meeting; consent faked; chiefs divided and paid; impact statements hidden; benefits to the connected while communities lose their land and sea.

**Who's supposed to guard against it.** The **Auditor-General** and the anti-corruption bodies examine public money and conduct. The **Environmental Advisory Committee** and the **courts** can revoke or quash a tainted consent. The **Public Solicitor** provides legal aid. And the Solomons' **civil society, churches, and press**, with regional and international watchdogs, have exposed the captured logging and mining trade.

**How to fight it safely.** Document precisely and factually — the faked consent, the hidden impact statement, who benefits, what the law required — and keep clear the line between what you can prove and what you suspect. Route serious evidence to the Auditor-General, the anti-corruption bodies, the Environmental Advisory Committee, the courts, and trusted journalists rather than making accusations you can't back. Protect those pressured, and pair the exposure with the consent fight.

North Korea is a mountainous, closed land of denuded slopes and flooding rivers, of coal and cold and hunger, where the forests were consumed hillside by hillside in a people's struggle to survive, and where the bare and eroded hills now unleash the floods that ruin the crops and deepen the hunger that drives still more cutting. This guide has hidden none of that — the near-total closure, the humanitarian root, the fraught cooperation, the absence of any domestic action or victory — and there is no promise of a swift or domestic healing. But even here the levers are real, and they run through the root and the outside: the agroforestry that plants fruit and nut trees on the barren hills to feed and fuel the people while holding the soil and breaking the flood-cycle at once; the reforestation cooperation that has, within limits, already greened some slopes; the satellite and scientific record that documents from afar what the closed state conceals; the international frameworks that carry what engagement is possible; and the diaspora that keeps the hidden crisis before the world's eyes. There is no domestic action, no reaching the closed state from within, and no work that does not demand the absolute protection of everyone inside; this guide has said so throughout, and made that protection its first and governing rule. But the forests and the floods and the hungry people of North Korea are worth every careful, external, root-centred effort, the levers that run through the agroforestry, the cooperation, and the science are real, and the patient, protective work of addressing the deforestation at its root, breaking the flood-and-famine cycle, and easing the poverty beneath it — from outside, and never at the cost of a single life inside — is one that the international bodies, the cooperators, the scientists, and the diaspora can genuinely, if slowly, do.

# HOW COMMUNITIES CAN STOP DESTRUCTIVE DEVELOPMENT PROJECTS IN THE CENTRAL AFRICAN REPUBLIC

# INTRODUCTION & FRAMEWORK

The Central African Republic is one of the world's poorest and most conflict-affected states — where armed groups and foreign paramilitaries hold much of the territory, where the resource wealth is captured by fighters, and where the safety of anyone inside must come before all else. Yet it holds a treasure of global importance: part of the **Congo Basin rainforest**, the second largest on earth, with its forest elephants gathering by the hundreds at famous clearings, its lowland gorillas, and the Indigenous forest peoples who have stewarded it for millennia. The same country is torn by a long conflict funded in part by its **diamonds and gold**, its forest pressed by **logging** and a poaching epidemic, and its resources caught up in arrangements that trade extraction for security. No one inside can safely resist. But the world has a genuine stake in this forest and its captured wealth, and the tools to act, from outside, are real.

Those who would defend the Central African Republic's environment cannot do so safely inside a conflict-torn land, but the tools from outside are real. The Congo Basin forest and its wildlife can be defended through **international and transboundary conservation** — the protected areas, the transboundary World Heritage stakes such as the Sangha Trinational, and the conservation partners who work the region. The diamonds and gold that fund the conflict can be pressed toward accountability through the **conflict-diamond and conflict-mineral certification and due-diligence frameworks**. The logging can be held to **international timber-legality and sustainability standards** in the world markets its wood reaches. The **capture of resources for security** — the arrangements that trade extraction for protection — can be documented and scrutinised through international investigative and transparency bodies. And the **international conservation, transparency, and humanitarian frameworks and partners** reach where domestic action cannot, while **rigorous documentation** grounds it all and needs no one inside. But these tools have grave limits, and this guide states them plainly. The conflict is active and much of the land dangerous, so open domestic opposition is impossible and perilous. The resource wealth is held by armed groups and foreign paramilitaries, so change is slow and external. The certification and conflict-mineral frameworks are real but imperfect and criticised. And, above all, no one inside can be exposed, and the conflict zones and armed actors must be avoided entirely. So the work must be external, conservation- and accountability-framed, protecting anyone inside absolutely — and the honest prize is the forest defended, the captured wealth held to account, and the harm documented, not a project stopped from within.

## The Whole Strategy in One Idea

You cannot safely resist inside a conflict-torn, resource-captured state, and you must never ask anyone to. What can be done is to defend the forest and hold its captured wealth to account, from outside: invoke the international and transboundary conservation of the Congo Basin, press the diamonds, gold, and timber through the certification, conflict-mineral, and legality frameworks, document the resource-for-security capture for the international bodies, and work through the conservation and humanitarian partners — while protecting everyone inside absolutely and avoiding the conflict zones entirely.

# HOW THE SYSTEM WORKS

## Who Actually Decides

Power over the resources rests with armed groups and foreign paramilitaries who hold the land, not with a functioning domestic system. The decisions resisters can reach are external — taken in the **international conservation bodies** that guard the forest, in the **certification and conflict-mineral frameworks** that govern diamonds and gold, in the **timber markets and legality regimes** abroad, and in the **international investigative, transparency, and humanitarian bodies** that scrutinise the capture. These, and the diaspora, are who can act from outside.

## How a Project Moves — and Where You Jump In

The extraction and the logging advance under the control of armed actors; there is no safe domestic hearing to contest. Resisters enter where the outside world touches the harm: the forest's conservation and World Heritage stakes, the diamonds' and gold's certification and due-diligence chains, the timber's markets, the international scrutiny of the resource-for-security arrangements. Each is a point of leverage reached from outside.

## Follow the Money and the Permissions

Follow the minerals: where the diamonds and gold flow, and which certification and due-diligence frameworks they must pass. Follow the timber: where the wood is sold, and what legality and sustainability standards those markets enforce. Follow the capture: which armed groups and paramilitaries trade extraction for security, and which international bodies can document and sanction it. The leverage lies in the certification chains, the timber standards, the conservation stakes, and the international scrutiny.

## Who's Supposed to Be Watching

Domestic oversight has collapsed in much of the country. The watchers resisters can engage are external: the international conservation bodies and the transboundary World Heritage system, the Kimberley-style diamond certification and conflict-mineral frameworks, the timber-legality regimes, the international investigative and transparency organisations, the humanitarian and human-rights bodies, and the diaspora. These are the institutions a careful, external case is built to reach.

# QUICK REFERENCE: SUCCESS RATES

No Central African effort yields a domestic "win," and honesty requires saying so — the resources are captured and the land is at war. What the external tools realistically deliver is defence and accountability: a treasured forest watched and its logging exposed, the conflict diamonds and gold pressed through certification, the timber's markets held to legality, the resource-for-security capture documented before the world. The single most decisive move is **rigorous, safely-gathered documentation**, because every external lever — the conservation bodies, the certification frameworks, the timber markets, the investigative organisations — responds to credible evidence, and because such evidence can be gathered without endangering anyone inside. Nothing here is fast, and no project is likely to be stopped from within. But a global forest and captured wealth give the world a genuine reason and duty to act.

A case built on satellite imagery, conservation data, and the certification chains — needing no one inside — is one that reaches the levers without endangering anyone.

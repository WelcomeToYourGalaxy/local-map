Effective efforts follow the same underlying pattern, whether the goal is to protect land and coast from a petroleum cluster, or to insist the nation's wealth be spent wisely:

```
STEP 1: TARGET IDENTIFICATION  (find the project, the harm, and the wealth question)
        |

        |
```

# STEP 1: TARGET IDENTIFICATION

Every effective effort begins by finding the exact project and harm — and the wealth question and the leverage the community holds in a real democracy. Vague concern goes nowhere, and pitting community against nation backfires; an effort aimed at "the supply base taking this farmland and coast, whose cost the analysts doubt, which the wealth debate and the institutions can scrutinise and the community's land rights can defend" is a real, workable effort — the kind Timor-Leste's open levers can deliver. Answer five questions.

## The Five Core Questions

**1. What would be lost?** This is the first question here. What **land, coast, farmland, fishing grounds, and livelihoods** would the project take — the hectares of community land, the salt farms, the fishermen's docks, the homes.

**2. Is the spending wise?** Whether the project is a **wise use of the nation's finite petroleum wealth** — the distinctive resource-governance question, since a costly work that never pays harms the whole nation.

**3. Which institutions and the wealth debate apply?** How the **parliament, the audit chamber, the courts, and the national Petroleum Fund debate** can scrutinise the project and its spending.

**4. Which civil society and the church apply?** Which **watchdog organisations** can analyse and expose the project, and how the **church** can lend moral weight.

**5. How are land rights and national pride held together?** How the **community's land rights** can be defended, and how opposition can be framed as **wise stewardship of the nation's wealth**, not as opposition to the nation.

# STEP 2: DOCUMENTATION — HOW TO BUILD AN UNASSAILABLE CASE

Documentation is the foundation everything else stands on — and here, where the case must persuade the institutions, the civil society, the church, and a proud public, sound evidence of both the harm and the doubtful cost is decisive. A parliamentarian, an auditor, a watchdog analyst, and a journalist all move on evidence — and in Timor-Leste, a clear record of the land and coast lost, and of the spending's doubtful wisdom, is precisely what an effort turns into a real result, so build it well. Build your file in three layers.

## Layer 1: The Project, the Cost, and the Deciders

Get the facts the case turns on. Identify the **project, its components, its budget, and the land and coast it would take**; and, crucially here, gather the evidence on its **cost and viability** — the analysts' and economists' judgements on whether it will ever recover its investment, and its effect on the Petroleum Fund. Establish the deciders and the doubtful economics precisely, because in Timor-Leste the distinctive case is not merely that a project harms a community, but that it may be an unwise use of the nation's finite and dwindling wealth.

## Layer 2: The Loss — Land, Coast, and Livelihood

Document what would be lost. Establish the **physical and environmental harm** — the farmland, salt farms, fishing grounds, and coast taken; the displacement; the pollution and health and ocean risks of an industrial petroleum complex — drawing on **the watchdog research, the environmental studies, and community knowledge**, much of which exists for a project as scrutinised as this one. And establish the **human harm** — the families displaced, the livelihoods and heritage lost. Photographs, maps, and testimony make the loss undeniable, and the cost evidence makes it a national question.

## Layer 3: The Loss, the Cost, the Rights, and the Wealth Question

Finally, document the case. Does the **environmental and human harm** show a real loss? Is the **spending unwise**, harming the nation's finite wealth? Whose **land rights** does it violate, and was there consent? And how does the case bear on the **Petroleum Fund and the nation's future**? Each is a separate element, and together they make a case that can be pressed in the institutions and the public square, and framed as wise national stewardship. This is the layer that most often decides these efforts, because a documented loss, a doubtful cost, a violated right, and a clear stake in the nation's wealth are exactly what let a Timorese community argue for wise and just development. Build it well, and the wealth debate, the institutions, the civil society, and land rights become the community's genuine strength. One practical note on building this layer: the doubtful cost is the element that most distinguishes a Timorese case and most repays careful work, because it is what elevates a local harm into a national question the whole democracy must weigh. A displacement or a land-taking, on its own, is a local matter a determined government can push through as the price of progress; but the same displacement, shown to be part of a megaproject that respected analysts judge unlikely ever to recover its cost — a project draining the nation's finite savings for works that may stand unused, as an airport already does, overgrown with weeds — becomes a question of national prudence that every citizen has reason to ask. So the file should establish, as rigorously as the evidence allows and drawing on the country's own capable analysts, exactly what the project will cost, what it is likely to return, and what its pursuit means for the Petroleum Fund and the nation's future. It is this economic case, more than the local harm alone, that gives a small community a claim on the whole nation's attention, and it is worth building with the greatest care.

# WHAT TO GATHER, AND WHERE TO FIND IT

Evidence in hand beats evidence you assume exists. Here is where the material tends to live in a Timorese effort, and how to get it — with the **harm**, the **cost**, the **land rights**, and the **wealth question** at the center. Treat this as a checklist to work through, not a menu to admire.

## The Project and Cost Record

Start with the project and its economics. **Project plans, budgets, contracts, and the Strategic Development Plan** establish what is intended and what it will cost; and, decisively here, gather the **analyses of the project's viability** — the economists' and watchdogs' judgements on whether it will recover its costs, and its effect on the Petroleum Fund. Much of this exists in the respected civil-society research and the economic studies on the megaproject, and is reachable in a country with a free press. Establish exactly **what the project is, what it costs, and whether it will ever pay**, because the doubtful economics are the distinctive lever.

## The Land, Coast, and Livelihood Evidence

This is the heart of the local file. Gather the **physical and environmental evidence** — the hectares of community land taken, the farmland and salt farms and fishing grounds lost, the coast and ocean at risk, the pollution and health hazards of an industrial petroleum complex — drawing on **maps, environmental studies, and the watchdog research**. And gather the **human evidence** — the families displaced, the livelihoods and customary and cultural uses lost. Photographs, maps, and testimony make the loss vivid and credible, and ground the national argument in real lives.

## The Wealth and Governance Record

Document the wealth question, because it is the distinctive Timorese lever. Gather the evidence on the **Petroleum Fund's decline**, the **end of the main producing field**, the **economists' calls to diversify**, and the **judgement that the megaproject may never pay** — so the case can show that the project risks the nation's finite savings and future. A community that can frame its local harm within the national question — is this a wise use of our dwindling wealth? — turns a local grievance into a national cause every citizen shares. This is worth dwelling on, because the Petroleum Fund occupies a place in Timorese national life that gives this framing unusual force. The Fund is not an abstract accounting entity but the tangible fruit of the nation's one great resource, carefully saved and widely understood as the inheritance meant to carry the country beyond the age of oil — the security of future generations in a poor nation with few other assets. To spend that inheritance down on a megaproject the experts doubt is therefore not merely a budgetary choice but a decision about whether the nation squanders or safeguards the future it fought for. A community that frames its fight in these terms — not as blocking a project but as guarding the nation's future against an imprudent gamble with its finite savings — speaks to something Timorese citizens feel deeply, and enlists the widest possible concern for what might otherwise seem a distant, local matter.

## The Rights and Consent Record

Document the land rights and consent. Gather the **community's land tenure and customary rights**, and whether **free, informed consent** was sought and given for the taking of land — since in a real democracy a project that violates rights or proceeds without genuine consent is vulnerable in the courts and the public square. And gather the record of any **displacement already done in anticipation** of the project. A community that can show its rights violated or its consent bypassed holds a strong, legitimate lever.

## The Allies and the Community

Finally, map the human terrain. Who is affected — the coastal and farming communities, the fishers, the displaced, the future? Who are the allies — the **watchdog organisations, the sympathetic parliamentarians, the church, the free press, the international observers**? And how is the community organised, and its cause framed as **wise national stewardship**? A Timorese effort is advanced through the community and its allies in an open democracy, framed as insisting on wise and just development — so map that coalition and those levers deliberately.

Some honest truths hold across every effort here. **The civil society and the resource-governance debate are the distinctive strengths, turning a local harm into a national question of wise stewardship.** **The open institutions — parliament, audit chamber, courts — are real and have checked petroleum contracts before.** **The community's land rights and the church's moral weight are legitimate and strong.** **But the project is wrapped in pride, the country is poor, and opposition must never be cast as anti-national.** Hold both truths at once: real, open, domestic democratic levers, and the honest weight of pride, poverty, and powerful backing.

Above all, keep the discipline — and the unity — that give these efforts their force. Document the harm and the doubtful cost. Engage the wealth debate, the parliament, the courts, and the audit institutions. Enlist the civil society and the church. Defend the community's land rights. And frame it all as wise national stewardship — because in a nation forged in sacrifice, an effort that defends the land and the finite wealth belonging to all, standing as the nation's prudent conscience, is the one that is both effective and just.

Timor-Leste is a young nation, poor but free, that bled for its independence and now stakes its future on its offshore gas — and along its southern coast a vast petroleum megaproject would take the farmland, the fishing grounds, and the salt farms of its people, and spend the nation's dwindling savings on works the experts doubt will ever pay, even as the main field runs dry; this guide has hidden none of it, nor pretended that the dream of running one's own industry is not deeply and understandably felt, and there is no landmark community victory here to promise you. But Timor-Leste is a real democracy, and that changes everything: the press is free, the civil society capable and respected, the parliament and courts and audit chamber open and real, the church a voice of conscience, and the debate over the nation's finite wealth one in which every citizen has a stake. There is no easy victory, and no landmark precedent to inherit; this guide has said so throughout, and insisted that the effort stand with the nation, not against it. But your land, your coast, your livelihoods, and your nation's hard-won wealth are worth defending, the open levers of your democracy are real and strong, and the patient work of documenting the harm, exposing the doubtful cost, engaging the parliament and the courts, and standing as your nation's prudent conscience is one your community can genuinely do — and win development that is wise, just, and worthy of a nation that bled to be free. So can you.

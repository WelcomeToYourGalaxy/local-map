Moldova is a small and not wealthy nation between the great river it calls the Nistru and the fields that feed it, a young democracy that has set its course for Europe, whose environmental life turns on a river most of its people drink from but which rises and is dammed beyond its borders, on forests among the scarcest in Europe, and on a soil and climate under growing strain. This guide has hidden none of that, nor pretended that a poor nation commands its own river, or that the road to Europe is short, and there is no single landmark victory to promise you. But Moldova holds something that many nations in this series do not: a real democracy, with courts that work, a civil society that is free, and a press that can speak — and, above that, the powerful leverage of a European accession that binds it to defend its environment by European law. Those are genuine levers, and they make the defence of the river, the forests, and the soil not a forlorn hope but a fair and often winnable fight, pursued openly and lawfully. There is no commanding the upstream river, and no shortcut to Europe; this guide has said so throughout. But the river and forests and soil of Moldova are worth every open, lawful effort, the levers that run through democracy and European leverage are real and strong, and the work of documenting the harm, invoking the standards, and using the open courts is one that Moldovans can genuinely do — and, as a democracy bound for Europe, win. So can you.

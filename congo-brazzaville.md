Beneath the swamp forests of the northern Congo lies the largest tropical peatland on earth, a store of carbon as vast as years of the whole world's emissions, sheltering gorillas and forest elephants and rare life, and sustaining the fishing and forest communities who have always lived there; and it is threatened by the drilling for oil beneath it, licensed on an assessment so old it predated the peatland's own discovery, in a poor and oil-dependent nation that fairly claims the same right to its resources that the rich world used to build its wealth. This guide has hidden none of that, nor pretended that the development need is not real, or that the sovereignty argument is not fair, or that a constrained state's political space is open, and there is no final victory here to promise you. But the very thing that makes this peatland's fate so grave is also the lever: because it holds carbon the whole planet needs kept in the ground, the whole planet has both the reason and the means to help — not by denying a poor nation its future, but by paying for a better one, so that keeping the carbon stored becomes the nation's better deal and the world's shared gain. There is no easy victory, and no escaping the fair claim of a poor nation to develop; this guide has said so throughout, and insisted on care for those who act and justice for those who need. But the peatlands and forests of the Congo are worth keeping whole, the levers that run through their global worth and the world's willingness to pay are real, and the patient, careful work of documenting the carbon, invoking the state's own word, and funding a better path is one that communities and their allies can genuinely do — from the swamp forest to the world — and keep a treasure of the whole earth intact. So can you.

Jordan is one of the driest nations on earth, a small kingdom where families queue for water a few hours a week, and where the gravest harms are done not by belching industry but by the mining of an irreplaceable ancient aquifer, the draining of a dying sea now pocked with sinkholes, the loss of a great wetland, and the wasting of water a thirsty nation cannot spare; this guide has hidden none of it, nor pretended that a state running out of water has easy choices, or that much of the harm is not beyond its borders, or that a kingdom's political space is open, and there is no landmark victory here to promise you. But the very thing that makes Jordan's predicament so grave is also the lever: because water is the nation's binding constraint and its foremost concern, a project that mines or wastes it is undermining the very security the state prizes above all, and can be pressed — on the state's own terms, with the state's own science, against the state's own strategy, and with a better way in hand. There is no easy victory, and no escaping the desperation of the crisis; this guide has said so throughout, and insisted on care for those who act. But the water of Jordan is worth protecting, the levers that run through a thirsty state's own deepest concern are real, and the patient, careful work of documenting the harm, making the water-security case, and offering the wiser use of a precious resource is one a community can genuinely do — from within the system, and press for the protection of Jordan's water. So can you.

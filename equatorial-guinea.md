And protect anyone inside absolutely — because for one of the world's most closed states, an effort that presses accountability and defends the environment from outside, through the world's courts, frameworks, and conscience, and that shields every person inside, is the one that is both effective and just.

Equatorial Guinea is a small nation of island and mainland forest that struck great wealth beneath its seas and might have turned it to the good of its people, but saw it captured by a few while the many stayed poor, its rainforest cleared, its coast poisoned, and its voice silenced by one of the world's severest repressions. This guide has hidden none of that, nor pretended that its people can safely oppose the capture at home, or that the entrenched hold is easily broken, and there is no landmark domestic victory to promise you. But the captured wealth reached into the wider world, and there it can be pursued; the rainforest is part of the world's forests, and there it can be defended; and the resource revenue is subject to the world's standards, and there it can be pressed toward the light. Those are the levers, and they are real: the international asset recovery that has already confiscated stolen wealth abroad, the transparency frameworks that reach the revenue, the conservation frameworks that defend the forest, and the world's courts and conscience that reach a closed state from outside. There is no safe domestic opposition, and no quick reckoning; this guide has said so throughout, and insisted above all on the absolute protection of anyone inside. But the wealth and forest and people of Equatorial Guinea are worth every careful, international effort, the levers that run through the world's courts, frameworks, and conscience are real, and the patient, external, protective work of documenting the capture, pursuing the wealth abroad, and defending the forest is one that the diaspora and the international community can genuinely do — and, from outside and with care, press accountability and defend the environment of a captured land. So can you.

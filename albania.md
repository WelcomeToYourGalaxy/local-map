# HOW COMMUNITIES CAN STOP DESTRUCTIVE DEVELOPMENT PROJECTS IN ALBANIA

*Complete Community Opposition How-To*

*This guide is built around Albania's distinctive, slightly counter-intuitive lever — shifting the whole fight from "stop this dam" to "protect the whole river," setting a positive, permanent goal the science justifies and the government can be won over to embrace — together with international and corporate partnership, biodiversity science, and court challenges. It names the tools you'll meet (the permanent-protection goal, the international campaign, the science, the courts, converting the government from proponent to protector) in plain words.*

# INTRODUCTION & FRAMEWORK

The **Vjosa** — one of the last free-flowing rivers in Europe, running more than four hundred kilometres from the mountains of Greece to the Adriatic, home to over a thousand species — was slated for a cascade of hydropower dams, the **Kalivaç** and the **Poçem** among them, part of a Balkan dam boom of thousands of planned projects. The government backed them; a foreign consortium held the concession. But Albanians would not lose their river. Riverside mayors and residents, together with the local group **EcoAlbania** and the international **Save the Blue Heart of Europe** campaign led by **Riverwatch** and **EuroNatur** — backed by scientists, by the outdoor company **Patagonia**, and by **IUCN** — did not merely fight the dams one by one. They documented the river's extraordinary life, challenged the dam permits in court and won, carried the fight to the world's media, and pressed a single, larger demand: not just stop the dams, but **protect the whole river as a national park**. Eight years on, in **March 2023**, the government declared the Vjosa a **Wild River National Park** — the first in Europe. A community that had been told a river was a site for dams had turned it, instead, into a protected wild river forever.

A community in Albania has real tools to work with, and the Vjosa campaign drew on most of them. **International and corporate partners** — the Save the Blue Heart of Europe campaign, IUCN, and values-driven companies like Patagonia — bring reach, standards, and resources. **Biodiversity science** supplies the foundation that justifies protection. **Court challenges** can strike at individual dam permits, as they did along the river; protest, petition, and litigation are lawful. And the lever that turned a run of defensive fights into a permanent win is the **designation itself** — a national park or protected area — which locks the outcome in for good once a **persuadable government** can be brought to grant it, converting the state from proponent to protector; that is how the Vjosa became Europe's first Wild River National Park. But these tools have real limits, and this guide states them plainly. The Vjosa win relied heavily on **international partners and a persuadable government** — the global campaign, Patagonia, IUCN, and a government willing to embrace the park for its own reasons, including tourism and prestige — leverage not always available; a community without such allies, or facing a government wholly set against protection, will find the model harder, and where those conditions are thin, the realistic gains are the **court challenge, the delay, and the record**. The win's **implementation is unfinished** — protection must be extended to tributaries and the delta, boundaries and management strengthened. It **protected one river** while the wider dam boom pressed on across other Balkan rivers. And Albania's **governance is weak** and its institutions corruptible, with development pressure — roads, an airport near the delta, tourism — continuing even beside a protected river. So aim for what this lever can deliver, knowing it works best where the value is outstanding and the government can be persuaded.

## The Whole Strategy in One Idea

A dam cascade looks unstoppable, but a river can be turned from a site for dams into a protected park, and a chain breaks at its weakest link. An Albanian fight turns on the **positive goal of permanent protection**; on **international and corporate partnership**; on **biodiversity science**; on **court challenges** to dam permits; and on **converting the government** from proponent to protector. Every one of those is a link you can grab — and the reframing to permanent protection is the one that saved the Vjosa. **Find the one closest to your hand and pull.**

# HOW THE SYSTEM WORKS

To break the chain you have to see it — who decides, how a project moves, and who can be won over.

Know which of these you can reach — the reframing to permanent protection and the international partnership are the sharpest.

**The international partnership.** *Your opening:* bring in the global campaign, IUCN, and corporate allies for reach, standards, and resources.

# STEP 1: TARGET IDENTIFICATION

Being against "the dam" is defensive and can lose the river. Being for "the whole river protected forever" is the winning frame. Answer five questions in writing.

**1. What is it, and who holds the concession?** Name the project (the dam or cascade), the government's role, and the foreign consortium and financiers.

**2. What outstanding value does the place hold?** The **free-flowing river, the species, the wild character** — because outstanding value draws international partners and justifies **permanent protection**, your winning frame.

**3. Can it be reframed to permanent protection?** Whether the fight can shift from "stop this dam" to "**protect the whole river as a national park**" — the decisive Vjosa move.

**4. What stage is it at, and what's the opening?** A concession, a permit (challenge it in court), a government persuadable by tourism and prestige. These are the openings.

**5. Who are your partners — and are they available?** **Riverside communities and mayors, local NGOs, scientists, international campaigns, IUCN, corporate allies** — and an honest read of whether such partners and a persuadable government are within reach.

**What this looks like in real life.** A cascade of dams is slated for a free-flowing river of outstanding value, the government backing it and a foreign consortium holding the concession. The five questions yield: the government and the consortium; the river's extraordinary value (drawing partners, justifying a park); a fight to reframe from "stop the dams" to "protect the whole river"; permits to challenge and a persuadable government; and international and corporate partners. That's the Vjosa pattern — reframe to permanent protection, build the partnership on biodiversity science, challenge the permits, and convert the government to protector.

# STEP 2: DOCUMENTATION

The difference between a community that can win permanent protection and one that can't is proof — of the river's outstanding value. Build it in three layers.

**Layer one: the biodiversity science.** The foundation: document the river's **extraordinary life** — the free-flowing character, the thousand species, the globally threatened ones — with **biodiversity science**, surveys, and expert reports. This is what justifies a national park and draws international partners, as the Vjosa's surveys did.

**Layer two: the permits and the harm.** Get the **dam permits and concessions**, and document the harm the dams would do — to challenge the permits in court and build the case.

**Layer three: the protection case and the government's incentive.** Shape the **positive case for permanent protection** — a national park the science justifies — and identify what would move the government (tourism, prestige, international standing). Walking into the courts, the international partners, or the government with the biodiversity science and a positive protection goal is what turns a defensive fight into a winning one.

# WHAT TO GATHER, AND WHERE TO FIND IT

- **The biodiversity science** — the free-flowing character, the species, the surveys — the foundation for a park.
- **The dam permits and concessions** — to challenge in court.
- **The harm the dams would do** — to build the case.
- **The protection case** — the positive goal of a national park the science justifies.
- **The government's incentive** — tourism, prestige, international standing — what converts it to protector.

# STEP 3: BUILDING LOCAL OPPOSITION

Documents give you a case; riverside communities plus international and corporate partners give you the alliance that saved the Vjosa. Build in four moves.

**Root it in the riverside communities and mayors.** The Vjosa campaign began with **riverside mayors and residents** holding a press conference on the bank and calling for a park. Root the effort there — the communities whose river it is.

**Build the international and corporate partnership.** The breadth of the alliance mattered enormously: a **global campaign** (Save the Blue Heart of Europe, Riverwatch, EuroNatur) brought experience and reach; **IUCN** brought the standards by which a place is judged worthy of protection; a **values-driven company** (Patagonia) brought resources and a worldwide platform; and **scientists** brought the evidence. A single village could summon none of these; together they turned a local grievance into a cause the government could not ignore.

**Set the positive goal and challenge the permits.** Shift the fight to **permanent protection — a national park** — the decisive reframing, and **challenge the dam permits in court** to strike them and build delay and record.

**Convert the government, and build to last.** Offer the government a **prize to claim** — a park it can embrace for tourism and prestige — converting it from proponent to protector. Build clear roles, a secure archive, a way to decide together, and the endurance for a campaign that, like the Vjosa's, may take the better part of a decade.

The Vjosa lever: shift the whole fight from "**stop this dam**" to "**protect the whole river as a national park**." The Vjosa's implementation is unfinished (tributaries, delta, boundaries), it protected one river while the dam boom pressed on, and Albania's governance is weak. # TURNING YOUR EVIDENCE INTO ARGUMENTS

A stack of documents wins nothing until you aim it — and the frame is everything.

**A dam fight becomes a case for a park.** Not "stop this dam" but *"protect this whole river forever, as Europe's wild-river treasure it is."*

**Biodiversity becomes worthiness.** *"This river holds over a thousand species and is one of Europe's last free-flowing rivers — it is worthy of a national park,"* the case IUCN's standards judge.

**A permit becomes a court case.** *"This dam permit is flawed — the courts can strike it,"* as the Vjosa campaign proved.

**A park becomes the government's prize.** *"Declare this a national park, and claim the tourism, the prestige, and the world's praise,"* turning proponent into protector.

Match it to the venue: the protection goal to the public and the government; the biodiversity science to IUCN and the partners; the permit flaws to the courts; the prize to the persuadable government.

# STEP 5: MEDIA STRATEGY

In Albania, international attention is a distinctive lever — the Vjosa became a global cause through Save the Blue Heart of Europe, Patagonia, and IUCN — and a persuadable government responds to world praise and prestige. International partners make the media front reach far.

**Lead with the wild river and the park, not procedure.** Not "the dam permit was flawed" but "one of Europe's last wild rivers, home to a thousand species — protect it forever as a national park." Use your strongest material: the biodiversity science, the river's beauty and life, and the riverside communities.

**Work several audiences at once.** Albanian press and communities build the local cause; international media, IUCN, and corporate partners reach the world, where the government wants praise. Make it easy: clear science, striking images of the river, the positive protection goal.

**Two hard rules.** Accuracy above all — one exaggeration can discredit the science. And lead with the positive goal — a river protected forever is a story to be for, which draws partners and offers the government a prize.

# WHEN THE SYSTEM IS TILTED

Albania's model is powerful, but it relies on conditions not always present, so plan for it.

**When you're fighting dams one by one.** Reframe. A defensive campaign can win battles and lose the river, because the proponent need only succeed once. Set the positive goal of permanent protection.

**When partners or a persuadable government are thin.** The Vjosa relied on both. Where they're thin, be honest: aim for the realistic gains — the court challenge, the delay, and the record — and keep building toward protection.

**When governance is weak and institutions corruptible.** Albania's are. Lean on the international partners, IUCN's standards, and world attention, which reach above weak domestic institutions, and on the courts to strike flawed permits.

**When implementation is unfinished.** The Vjosa park still needs its tributaries, delta, and management secured. A declaration is a beginning; keep pressing to make protection real and complete.

# WHEN IT'S ACTUAL CORRUPTION — AND WHO'S CAPTURED

Albania's dam boom, its concessions to foreign consortia, and its weak, corruptible institutions make capture a real risk, so this matters.

**How to spot it.** Concessions and permits to the connected; assessments that ignore the river's value; a dam boom pushed regardless of harm; benefits to the connected while communities lose their river.

**Who's supposed to guard against it.** Albania's audit and oversight bodies examine public money and conduct. The **courts** can strike a flawed permit. **International partners, IUCN, and the EU-accession process** bring standards and scrutiny from above. And Albania's **local NGOs (like EcoAlbania), riverside communities, and international campaigns** can expose a bad deal.

**How to fight it safely.** Document precisely and factually — how a concession or permit ignored the river's value, who benefits — and keep clear the line between what you can prove and what you suspect. Route serious evidence to the courts, the international partners, the EU-accession monitors, and trusted journalists rather than making accusations you can't back. Pair the exposure with the permanent-protection campaign.

# INTEGRATION & TIMELINE

Don't run these steps one after another — run them together. The biodiversity science builds your case; the reframing to permanent protection aims it; the riverside communities and international partners are the muscle; the court challenges and the government's incentive press it; world attention carries it. Together they multiply. One at a time, or purely defensively, they lose the river — and the fight takes years.

**Early on (year 1):** root the effort in the riverside communities; document the biodiversity; reframe to permanent protection; begin building international and corporate partnership.

**In the thick of it (years):** challenge the dam permits in court; grow the global campaign; press the biodiversity case to IUCN's standards; offer the government the prize of a park.

**The long haul (years):** convert the government from proponent to protector; secure the declaration; then press to complete implementation — tributaries, delta, boundaries; hold the line for a river protected permanently.

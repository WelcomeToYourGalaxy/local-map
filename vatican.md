# HOW COMMUNITIES CAN STOP DESTRUCTIVE DEVELOPMENT PROJECTS IN VATICAN

*Complete Community Opposition How-To*

# INTRODUCTION & FRAMEWORK

Environmental advocacy in the Vatican is unlike that of any other country in this series, and it is important to explain why at the outset. The Vatican is the world's smallest sovereign state, a walled enclave of less than half a square kilometre within Rome, with almost no territory, industry, or development of its own to protect. Its environmental significance lies instead in its role as the sovereign seat of the Catholic Church: the source of one of the world's most influential bodies of environmental teaching — the encyclical *Laudato Si'* (2015) and its follow-up *Laudate Deum* (2023) — a centre of global moral leadership, and a state that has pledged to become the first carbon-neutral country. Accordingly, this guide is not about opposing development within the Vatican itself, where there is little to oppose, but about engaging with the Vatican's distinctive environmental role: advancing the Church's environmental teaching, supporting and holding the Vatican to its carbon-neutral commitments, mobilising the worldwide Catholic community to care for the environment in their own countries, conserving the Vatican's heritage and gardens, and encouraging the Holy See's diplomatic and interfaith cooperation on environmental issues. While the Vatican itself is tiny and possesses little territory of its own to protect, and while the global Church encompasses diverse views, its environmental influence lies in leadership, example, and its ability to inspire action far beyond its borders. As its landmark teaching and its carbon-neutral commitment show, these are practical and significant avenues for environmental advocacy.

Two points frame everything that follows. First, as in every guide, no single lever does the work alone; the results come from combining them. Second — and particular to the Vatican — the levers here are moral and global rather than territorial: the teaching, the carbon-neutral example, the worldwide community of the faithful, the heritage and gardens, and the Holy See's diplomacy. This guide is written for anyone — in the worldwide Catholic community, among the Church's institutions or the Vatican's environmental offices, or working with the Holy See's diplomacy — who wants to know what can actually be done with the moral leadership of the world's smallest state. Its governance is singular and is treated throughout with neutral respect.

The Vatican is not an ordinary territory with development to oppose, the global Church does not act as one, and a tiny state's own emissions are not what matter — the significance is leadership and example. This guide states those limits plainly, works through the teaching, the example, the community, and the diplomacy, and keeps neutral respect for the Church's governance.

# STEP 4: MORAL, INSTITUTIONAL & EXAMPLE-BASED CHALLENGES — THE TEACHING, THE EXAMPLE, AND THE COMMON HOME

## The Teaching and the Moral Voice

The Church's environmental teaching — *Laudato Si'* on the care of our common home and *Laudate Deum* on the climate crisis — carries moral authority to a community of well over a billion. The lever is to draw on it and amplify it: translate its call into real action across the worldwide community, amplify its voice on creation and the climate in the world's conversation, and hold the Church and the Vatican faithfully to the teaching they themselves proclaim. How you actually do this: the Vatican office responsible for *Laudato Si'* is the **Dicastery for Promoting Integral Human Development** (humandevelopment.va), and the worldwide network built to carry the teaching into action is the **Laudato Si' Movement** (laudatosimovement.org) — over 900 Catholic organisations and thousands of trained volunteers. Bring the teaching to life where you are by joining or starting a **Laudato Si' Circle** (a small local group), or training as a **Laudato Si' Animator** (animators@laudatosimovement.org) to organise your parish or school.

## The Example and the Carbon-Neutrality

The Vatican has pledged to become the world's first carbon-neutral state, acceded to the Paris Agreement and UNFCCC in 2022, installed solar since 2008, moved toward electric vehicles, and launched a large agrivoltaic solar plant at Santa Maria di Galeria (the *Fratello Sole* project) intended to power the whole state. The lever is to support and uphold that example — encouraging the completion of the solar and renewable projects and the wider greening, and holding it up as a model. Its weight is out of all proportion to the tiny state's actual emissions: the Vatican's own footprint is negligible in global terms, but the significance lies not in the tonnage saved but in the demonstration made — that an institution of the Vatican's visibility has judged the care of creation important enough to reorder its own energy around it. That is a beacon no small state's emissions could ever be: proof that the care of our common home can be lived, the smallest of states showing what it asks of the largest. How you actually do this: the concrete on-ramp is the **Laudato Si' Action Platform** (laudatosiactionplatform.org), the Vatican's own programme (run by the Dicastery for Promoting Integral Human Development) where a parish, school, diocese, religious order, business or family enrols, commits to sustainability goals, and records a real plan — the structure that turns the Vatican's one example into thousands of local ones.

## The Global Church and the Common Home

The true reach of the Vatican's environmental weight is the worldwide community it seats — well over a billion people, and a vast network of dioceses, parishes, schools, hospitals, charities, and lay movements across nearly every country. This is a reach no other actor in this series commands. The lever is to mobilise that community to act for the environment where they are — tree-planting, clean energy, stewardship in a hundred countries at once — to green the Church's own worldwide institutions, and to join with other faiths in the shared care of creation, so that a teaching proclaimed in the smallest of places becomes action in the largest number of them. How you actually do this, step by step: join or start a **Laudato Si' Circle**; become a **Laudato Si' Animator** (animators@laudatosimovement.org) to enthuse your parish and school; enrol your community — parish, school, diocese, order, or family — in the **Laudato Si' Action Platform** (laudatosiactionplatform.org) and log a plan of concrete steps (solar panels, tree-planting, waste and water); and mark the **Season of Creation** each year (1 September to 4 October) with prayer and action. These are the actual doors through which "mobilise the community" stops being a phrase and becomes solar panels, gardens and tree-planting in a hundred countries at once.

The golden rule: never misrepresent the teaching, never overstate the tiny state's own weight, and keep neutral respect for the governance. Credibility rests on a faithful grounding in the teaching and the commitment.

Media does three things here: it carries the teaching to the worldwide community, holds up the carbon-neutral example for the world, and reaches the global and interfaith audiences drawn to the Vatican's outsized environmental voice. Match the channel to the audience — the Church's own communications and community networks for the faithful; global and environmental media for the carbon-neutral bid; interfaith and international forums for the shared care of creation; diplomatic and climate channels for the moral voice. # WHEN THE TEACHING OUTRUNS THE PRACTICE — HOLDING TO ITS OWN WORD

There is no question of the classic corruption other guides confront. The honest counterpart is the gap that can open, in any institution however well-intentioned, between fine teaching and pledges and their full living-out — the risk that the landmark teaching and the carbon-neutral commitments are proclaimed more than achieved. Its marks are specific and named respectfully: a teaching proclaimed but little translated into action; carbon-neutral pledges made but slowly fulfilled; worldwide institutions slow to green in line with the teaching.

The response runs through transparency, fidelity, encouragement, and example. Document the teaching, the commitments, and their progress faithfully; use the published teaching and the stated commitments to encourage their fuller living-out; use the carbon-neutral example and its progress to encourage completion; use the worldwide community to translate the teaching into action where the institution's own pace is slow. Holding an institution faithfully to its own teaching is fair and, here, exactly what the teaching itself calls for. Throughout, keep neutral respect for the singular governance, keep the focus wholly on the care of the environment, and never stray into the doctrinal or political — encourage rather than accuse.

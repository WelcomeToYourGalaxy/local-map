Qatar is a small, rich peninsula on the Arabian Gulf, a desert nation built on the world's largest gas field and dependent on the sea it must desalinate to drink, one of the world's highest per-capita carbon emitters, reclaiming its shallow Gulf and straining to feed itself from a barren land. This guide has hidden none of that — the closed society, the economy-defining gas, the structural carbon, the existential desalination — and there is no promise of grassroots victory of the ordinary kind. But Qatar is a high-capacity state that has made real commitments, and its levers are genuine and, once moved, substantial: the efficiency, the solar, and the carbon capture Qatar has itself pledged, that can green the gas and cut the emissions; the desalination efficiency, the renewable power, and the reuse that can make an existential water system lighter on the climate and the Gulf; the marine protection that can defend the seagrass and dugongs of a stressed and globally important sea; and the water-efficient agriculture that can reconcile the food and the scarce water.

Iran is an ancient land of great rivers, storied lakes, and forests older than memory, grown over decades into a nation draining its own waters faster than the earth can replace them — its greatest lake a bed of salt, its famous river dry through a historic city, its plains sinking, its air choked with the dust of the waters it has lost. This guide has hidden none of that, nor pretended that the drought alone is to blame, or that the space is open and the work safe, and there is no landmark victory to promise you. But the crisis is one the state itself has come to acknowledge as existential, and there the technical case for reform can stand; the ancient forests are the world's as well as the nation's, and there they can be defended; and the science of the loss and the saving is strong and Iranian, and there it carries weight. Those are the levers, and they are real: the state's own recognition of water bankruptcy, the strong hydrological science that measures the mismanagement and its remedies, the world's heritage frameworks that reach the forests, and the visible, deadly air crisis that ties the cities' suffering to the drying waters. There is no open opposition in a state where environmental work has led to imprisonment, and no quick reversal of a decades-deep crisis; this guide has said so throughout, and insisted above all on the protection of everyone involved. But the waters and forests and air of Iran are worth every careful, scientific effort, the levers that run through the state's own crisis recognition and the strength of the science are real, and the patient, technical, protective work of documenting the harm, pressing the acknowledged crisis, and defending the forests is one that those who care can genuinely do — and, carefully and on the science, help a great and ancient land turn back toward its waters. So can you.

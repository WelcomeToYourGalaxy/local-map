And yet the effort is not futile, because the levers here, though wholly external, are real: the mining and its labour can be held to account through the foreign companies, the international courts, and the frameworks, as the landmark lawsuit proved; the land's greening can be honestly weighed and supported from outside; the water and the Red Sea can be stewarded through international and scientific cooperation; and the external, international, scientific, and diaspora channels are the real avenues a closed state leaves open. Those levers of the mines, the land, the water, the sea, and the external channels are real.

Some honest truths hold across every effort here. **The mining's accountability is real but wholly external — with the foreign companies, provenly reachable by the courts.** **There is no safe domestic space, so the work is wholly external and the protection of anyone inside governs all.** **The greening is real yet built on coerced labour — credited and named with equal honesty.** **And the diaspora is the voice a silenced people cannot raise at home.** Hold both truths at once: real, external levers of the corporate accountability, the courts, the science, and the diaspora, and the grave weight of a wholly closed and repressive state.

Above all, keep the discipline — and the absolute protection of everyone inside — that give these efforts their force. Document the impact and the labour from outside. Hold the mining and its labour to account through the foreign companies, the courts, and the frameworks. Weigh the greening honestly. Steward the water and the Red Sea through international and scientific cooperation. Use the corporate-accountability, international, scientific, and diaspora channels. And protect every person inside above all — because against one of the world's most closed states, an effort that holds the mining to account and stewards the environment wholly from outside, centred on the external accountability and keeping every person inside from harm, is the one that is both effective and just.

Eritrea is a Horn-of-Africa nation of highland terrace and Red-Sea coast and Danakil desert, one of the world's most closed and repressive states, where the economy is built on foreign-run mines documented to use coerced national-service labour, where the land is greened by that same labour, and where the environment is hidden from the world and its people cannot defend it openly at home. This guide has hidden none of that — the near-total closure, the coerced labour, the economy-defining mining, the absence of any safe domestic space — and there is no swift or landmark victory, and no safe domestic movement, to promise you. But the levers here, though wholly external, are real and have already been proven: the accountability of the foreign companies that run the mines, held answerable at home by a landmark lawsuit for what is done in Eritrea; the international courts and frameworks; the scientific and satellite monitoring that sees what the closed state hides; and the large diaspora that can act and speak safely abroad for a homeland that cannot. There is no safe domestic space, no way to challenge the mining from within, and no work that does not demand the absolute protection of everyone inside; this guide has said so throughout, and insisted above all on the safety of every person inside one of the world's most repressive states. But the mines and land and water and sea of Eritrea, and the people who bear their cost, are worth every careful, external, protective effort, the levers that run through the foreign companies, the courts, the science, and the diaspora are real and proven, and the patient, wholly external work of holding the mining to account, weighing the greening honestly, and stewarding the water and sea — while every person inside is protected above all — is one that the international community, the accountability frameworks, the scientists, and the Eritrean diaspora can genuinely do. So can you.

# STEP 2: DOCUMENTATION — HOW TO BUILD AN UNASSAILABLE CASE

Documentation is the foundation everything else stands on — and here, uniquely, the decisive evidence is the measurement of radiation, which independent science can establish beyond any denial. Get the facts the case turns on — measured. Document the harm, thoroughly. Measurements, photographs, and testimony make the harm undeniable, and the fuel chain makes it a European responsibility.

## Layer 3: The Harm, the Fuel Chain, the Clean-Up, and the Safety

Finally, document the case and the plan. Does the **measured contamination** prove a real, radioactive harm? How is it **tied to the company and the fuel chain** that fuels Europe's reactors? One practical note on building this layer: the independent, third-party nature of the radiation measurement is what most distinguishes a Nigerien case and most repays careful attention, because a measurement's power depends entirely on its credibility. A company or a state that owns the mines has every incentive to produce reassuring figures, and a community's own untrained readings, however sincere, can be dismissed as alarmist or mistaken. But a measurement taken by an independent, credentialed radiation laboratory — one with no stake in the outcome, using recognised methods, publishing its data — carries an authority that neither the company nor the state can easily wave away, precisely because it comes from outside the interested parties. This is why connecting to the established independent radiation laboratories is among the most valuable things a community can do: it is not merely gathering evidence but gathering evidence of a kind that cannot be dismissed. The file should therefore be built, wherever possible, on independent, third-party measurement, because it is that independence, as much as the measurement itself, that gives the evidence its unshakeable force.

Niger holds beneath its northern desert some of the world's richest uranium, and for half a century that ore was drawn from the earth to fuel the reactors of France and Europe, while the land around the mining towns was left with water poisoned above safe limits, streets and homes made radioactive, and tens of millions of tonnes of radioactive waste lying open to the wind; this guide has hidden none of it, nor pretended that uranium is not central to a poor nation's economy, or that the state which has now taken the mines will readily curb them, and there is no landmark community victory here to promise you. But the very thing that makes this harm so consequential — that Niger's uranium powers the "clean" nuclear energy Europe prides itself on — is the lever: the reactors and the company that carry responsibility back to the source, the independent science that measures the radiation beyond any denial, and the clean-up that a poisoned people is owed. There is no easy victory, and no landmark precedent to inherit; this guide has said so throughout, and insisted, above all, on the safety of your people. But your water, your health, your land, and your children's future are worth defending, the levers that reach from the poisoned desert to the reactors of Europe are real, and the patient, careful work of measuring the contamination, holding the company and the fuel chain to account, and demanding the decontamination of your land is one your community can genuinely do — and press for the healing of a poisoned land. So can you.

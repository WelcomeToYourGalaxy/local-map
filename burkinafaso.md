Burkina Faso is a landlocked Sahelian nation, a land of gold and cotton and hard-won soil, military-led and gripped by insecurity, where hundreds of thousands dig for gold to survive while mercury and cyanide seep into the water, and where, against the encroaching desert, its own farmers have pushed back the sands with the humble zaï pit and the stone bund. This guide has hidden none of that — the fraught state, the insecurity, the entangled gold, the survival poverty — and there is no swift or landmark victory to promise you.

Cuba guards some of the Caribbean's finest reefs and coasts with a care few nations match, and writes environmental law as strong as any in the region; yet across its northern cays it has thrown long stone causeways that choke the bays behind them, turning the water to salt and killing the mangroves by the square kilometre to carry beach tourism to the keys, and in its eastern nickel country it has exempted its mines from the very law that is otherwise its pride; this guide has hidden none of it, nor pretended that a one-party state which drives and profits from these projects will readily curb them, or that a people with little room for open dissent can oppose them freely, and there is no landmark community victory here to promise you. But the very things that make Cuba's harm a paradox are the levers: a state that writes strong environmental law can be held to it, a state that funds excellent conservation science cannot easily dismiss its own data, a state proud of its protected reefs can be shamed by the mangroves it lets die, and a state that sells its nature to the world has a reason to protect it. There is no easy victory, and no landmark precedent to inherit; this guide has said so throughout, and insisted on care for those who act. But the coasts and reefs and mangroves of Cuba are worth defending, the levers that run through a conservation-proud state's own commitments are real, and the patient, careful work of documenting the harm with the state's own science, holding it to its own law, and invoking the conservation it is proud of is one a community can genuinely do — from within the system, and press for the protection of Cuba's coasts. So can you.
